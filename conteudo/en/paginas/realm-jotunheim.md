## Land of the giants

Jotunheim (Jötunheimr, “the world of the giants”) is the wild region where the jötnar, the giants, come from. It lies far from the human world, to the east, and the poem Vafþrúðnismál says that the river Ífingr, which never freezes, divides the land of the gods from the land of the giants.

The giants descend from Ymir. When Odin, Vili and Vé killed him, his blood flooded the world and drowned almost all of them. Only Bergelmir and his wife escaped in a boat, and from them came the giants of later days.

## Gods on a visit

Almost every time a god crosses into Jotunheim there is danger or a trick. Thor travelled to Útgarðr, the fortress of Útgarða-Loki, and seemed to fail every test: he could not lift the “cat” (really the Midgard Serpent), could not drain the horn (which was linked to the sea) and could not win a wrestling match against an old woman (who was old age itself). The giant king had used illusions.

In another tale, the giant Þrymr stole Thor’s hammer and would return it only in exchange for Freyja. Thor went dressed as the bride. And the giantess Skaði, daughter of Þjazi, came down to Asgard in armor to avenge her father and ended up married to the god Njörðr.

## The Iron Wood

East of Midgard lies the Járnviðr, the Iron Wood, where an old giantess breeds wolves. According to the Völuspá and the Gylfaginning, from her come the offspring that will chase the Sun and the Moon, and the line of the wolf Fenrir.

## Trolls and peasant tales

In the medieval sources, “troll” is a vague word for giants, monsters and sorceresses. In Scandinavian folk tradition it got a face of its own: a big, clumsy creature of the mountains and forests that turns to stone if the sun touches it. The tales collected in Norway by Asbjørnsen and Moe, from 1841 on, made famous Askeladden, the clever lad of the ashes, and the Three Billy Goats Gruff, who outwit the troll under the bridge. Some stories on Boreal Saga Play live in this world.
