## Realm of fire

Muspelheim (Múspellsheimr) is the world of fire, in the south. The Prose Edda describes it as bright and hot, so scorching that those who are not native to it cannot endure it. At its border stands Surtr, who guards it with a flaming sword. The origin of the word Múspell is uncertain.

## Where fire meets ice

Before there was earth, sky or sea, there was only Ginnungagap, the void. To the north lay Niflheim, cold and misty. To the south, Muspelheim. The heat from the south met the frost from the north, the ice began to melt, and from the drops Ymir, the first giant, was born. Life came from the meeting of fire and ice, says Snorri.

## The sparks in the sky

After killing Ymir and making the world from his body, Odin, Vili and Vé took sparks and embers flying out of Muspelheim and fixed them in the sky, to light heaven and earth. This is how, according to the Prose Edda, the stars, the Sun and the Moon came to be.

## The day the fire returns

In Ragnarök, the sons of Muspell ride behind Surtr, and the bridge Bifröst breaks under them. The Völuspá says that Surtr comes from the south with “the scourge of branches” (fire), and the Gylfaginning tells that he flings fire over the earth and burns the whole world. It is the end of the old world. But after the fire, the Völuspá sees the earth rise green from the sea.
