## How to play

Scandinavians of the 11th century carved runes into a marble lion, now in front of the Arsenal in Venice. Sixteen stones with runes appear face up for a few seconds and then turn over. Tap two stones to turn them up: if they carry the same rune, the pair stays up. If they are different, both turn back and that is a mistake. Find the 8 pairs before time runs out. Nine mistakes end the game.

## The story behind it

The marble lion from Piraeus, in Venice, has three runic inscriptions from the 11th century. The runes on the stones in the game, the pairs and the rhythm are invention. Read the story in [The Piraeus Lion: the runes Vikings carved on a Greek marble lion, now in Venice](/en/2027/04/04/o-leao-do-pireu-as-runas-que-vikings-gravaram-num-leao-de-marmore-grego-hoje-em-veneza/).
