Among the strange creatures of Norse mythology, there is a stag that stands on top of a hall of dead warriors. His name is Eikþyrnir, and almost everything we know of him comes from two stanzas of a poem of the Poetic Edda, the Grímnismál.

## The stag on the roof

The Grímnismál describes Valhalla, the house of Óðinn where the warriors chosen for the battle at the end of the world live. Above the hall, says the poem, stands a stag called Eikþyrnir, who bites the branches of a tree called Læraðr. The name Eikþyrnir is usually explained as "oak thorn" or something similar, but the exact reading is uncertain.

## The drops from the antlers

What makes the stag famous is what falls from him. From his antlers, according to the poem, drops fall into a spring called Hvergelmir. From there flow all the rivers of the world. The Grímnismál gives a list of names of these rivers, and the Gylfaginning, Snorri Sturluson's work on mythology, repeats the idea.

## Hvergelmir

Hvergelmir is one of the springs of Norse cosmology. According to Snorri, it lies under one of the roots of the world tree, Yggdrasil, in the cold of the north, and the serpent Níðhöggr lives there. The image is of a world in which high and low touch: the stag on the roof of Valhalla, the spring among the roots, and the water that links the two.

## A difficult text

Scholars debate whether Læraðr is another name for Yggdrasil or a separate tree, and the text does not settle the doubt. Even so, the stag is a strong image: an animal that eats leaves and lets fall water that becomes rivers.
