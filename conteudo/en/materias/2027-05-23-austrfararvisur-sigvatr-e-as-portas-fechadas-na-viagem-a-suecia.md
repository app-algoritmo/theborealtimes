Austrfararvísur, "verses of the journey east", is a skaldic poem in dróttkvætt by Sigvatr Þórðarson, composed around 1019. It tells, with sarcasm, of a diplomatic mission sent by Olaf Haraldsson to King Olof of Sweden. The journey reconciled the two kings, and the agreement was sealed by the engagement of Olaf II to Astrid Olofsdotter.

## The journey

The poem describes the hard crossing of Eidskogen, the border forest, into Västergötland. Sigvatr reaches a farm called Hof and finds the door barred. At two other farms he is also turned away.

## What the women say

A woman of the house says they are heathens and that she fears Odin's wrath. Another says that inside they are holding an álfablót, a sacrifice to the elves, and she cannot let a stranger in. Sigvatr replies with irony, describing the reception and the people.

Verses 4 to 6, in Fulk's edition, are the most quoted of this scene.

## Who Sigvatr was

Sigvatr Þórðarson (995 to 1045) served Olaf II, Cnut, Magnus the Good and Anund Jacob. He was stallare, a kind of marshal, and baptised Olaf's son Magnus. About 160 of his verses survive. Known poems include Víkingarvísur, from around 1014 and 1015, Nesjavísur, on the battle of Nesjar in 1016, Vestrfararvísur, Tryggvaflokkr, Bersöglisvísur, Erfidrápa Óláfs helga and Knútsdrápa.

## In short

The poem is a travel account in verse, with humour about a Sweden still tied to the old rites. To cross the road and dodge the closed doors, play [Sigvatr's closed doors](/game-sigvatr-s-closed-doors/).
