Sólarljóð, "the Song of the Sun", is a poem written in Iceland around 1200, in the ljóðaháttr metre of the Poetic Edda poems. The content, however, comes from Christian visionary literature.

## Who speaks

The poem is anonymous, but some manuscripts attribute it to Sæmund. It has 82 stanzas, and some manuscripts carry a stanza 83, though few do. The speaker is a father who has already died and addresses his son from the afterlife. He reveals his own identity only in the last stanzas.

## The parts of the poem

According to Gro Steinsland's division:

- Stanzas 1 to 24: examples of the lives, deaths and fates of anonymous people.
- Stanzas 25 to 32: advice similar to that of the Hávamál.
- Stanzas 33 to 38: a "psychological biography" of the narrator.
- Stanzas 39 to 45: the "sun stanzas" that give the poem its name.
- Stanzas 46 to 56: the narrator in a limbo between life and death.
- Stanzas 57 to 80: hell and heaven, often compared to Dante's Divine Comedy.
- Stanzas 81 and 82: an address to the son and the hope of resurrection.

## Pagan or Christian?

There are references to Norse mythology in the poem, but it shows no sign of syncretism and has a firmly Christian outlook. Fredrik Paasche argues that the material from the pagan age is used to convey a worldview that is not pagan. Peter Hallberg has also discussed the poem's heritage and structure.
