In Iceland in the time of the sagas, when families gathered in summer for the parliament or for feasts, there was time for games. One of them was knattleikr, a word that means something like "ball game" (from knöttr, "ball"). It appears in several sagas, and it is one of the most discussed team sports of the period.

## How it was played

The sagas give no rulebook, so what we know is an incomplete picture. Two sides faced each other on a field or, in some accounts, on the ice. The players used a wooden bat, the knatttré, to send a ball, and could also contest it with their bodies. Some researchers compare the game with sports that still exist in the British Isles, such as Scottish shinty and Irish hurling, but that comparison is always approximate.

## Strength and fighting

The game was rough, and the sagas say so without shame. In Egils saga, the boy Egill, about seven years old, plays against an older and bigger boy, and, after losing a contest and being hurt, attacks him with an axe. The scene shows that games were a place where honour was at stake, and where fame and shame travelled fast in front of the public.

## A game for everyone

Knattleikr was also a way for young people to show their strength and to be seen by neighbouring families. Those who played well won respect, and those who lost could carry the memory for years. Many of the great quarrels in the sagas begin in places like this, and the ball is only the first pretext.
