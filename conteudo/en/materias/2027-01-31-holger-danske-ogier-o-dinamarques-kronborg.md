Holger Danske, in the French form Ogier the Dane, is a character who crosses centuries and countries: he begins as a knight of Charlemagne in the French songs and becomes, in Denmark, the hero who sleeps and wakes when the country is in danger. The source consulted for this piece gathers the works in which he appears, the legend of Kronborg and the discussions about a possible historical model. This piece separates what the works record from what is tradition.

## From the French songs

The first appearance is in the *Chanson de Roland*, around 1060, where he is called Oger. Around 1200 to 1215, Raimbert de Paris composed *La Chevalerie Ogier de Danemarche*, a poem of about 13,000 lines devoted to him. In the 14th century, Ogier appears in Avalon, linked to Morgan le Fay. Around 1240, the *Karlamagnús saga*, in Old Norse, calls him Oddgeir danski.

## In the Danish tradition

The Danish tradition appears in texts such as that of Christiern Pedersen, of 1534. The legend of the hero asleep beneath Kronborg castle was recorded by Keightley, in 1834, by Thiele, in 1843, and by Andersen, in 1845. According to this legend, Holger wakes when Denmark needs him.

## Art and memory

The figure inspired an opera by Kunzen, of 1789, a text by Ingemann, of 1837, and a statue by Hans Peder Pedersen-Dan, of 1907. In the Second World War, a Danish resistance group took the name Holger Danske.

## The path in brief

In dates, the course is this: around 1060, the *Chanson de Roland*; from 1200 to 1215, Raimbert's poem; around 1240, the Norse saga; in the 14th century, Avalon; in 1534, the text of Christiern Pedersen; in 1789, the opera; from 1834 to 1845, the versions of the Kronborg legend; in 1837, Ingemann; in 1907, the statue. The figure changes function along the way: from knight of a foreign emperor he becomes the sleeping guardian of Denmark.

## A historical model?

The source mentions names that scholars have proposed as a historical model: Autcharius, Audacar, Adalgis and Othgerius. The choice between them is disputed.

## Terms

A *chanson de geste* is a medieval French song of heroic deeds. The *Chanson de Roland* is the most famous of them. *Kronborg* is the Danish castle, in Elsinore. The *Karlamagnús saga* is the Norse version of the stories of Charlemagne. A *sleeping hero* is the type of legend in which a hero sleeps waiting for the hour to return.

## What remains uncertain

Holger's historical model is disputed, and none of the names is accepted by all. The tradition that the guides of Kronborg tell the legend today is not a documented fact about the castle: the source presents it as tradition. Nor can it be said, on the basis of this source, that the Kronborg legend is as old as the French songs: it is recorded by writers of the 19th century.
