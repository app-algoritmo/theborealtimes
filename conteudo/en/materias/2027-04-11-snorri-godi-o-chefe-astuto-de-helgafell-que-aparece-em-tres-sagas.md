Snorri goði, born in 963 and died in 1031 according to the sources, was an Icelandic chieftain. *Goði* was the title of someone who held local power and presided at the Althing. He is the main character of the *Eyrbyggja saga* and appears in two other sagas. The portrait changes from one to the next.

## In the Eyrbyggja saga

Snorri buys Helgafell from his uncle and stepfather, Börkr the Stout, after Börkr's divorce from Þórdís, and Börkr has to leave (chapter 14). The saga describes him as "a very shrewd man with unusual foresight, a long memory and a taste for vengeance" (chapter 15). When Iceland converted to Christianity, in 1000, Snorri had a church built at Helgafell (chapter 49). After Bolli Þorleiksson was killed, Snorri and Guðrún Ósvífursdóttir swapped properties: she moved to Helgafell, and he to Sælingsdalstunga (chapter 56).

## In the Laxdæla saga

The *Laxdæla saga* shows Snorri later, helping to arrange peace between the Ólafssons and Guðrún's sons (chapter 71). The property swap with Guðrún, however, comes from the *Eyrbyggja saga* (chapter 56), not from this one.

## In Njáls saga

In *Njáls saga*, Snorri appears after the burning of Njáll. At the Althing he backs Njáll's kinsmen, stops the retreat of Flosi Þórðarson and helps, with the Lawspeaker Skapti Þóroddsson, to separate the two sides. He is then chosen to help arrange the settlement (chapter 145). Earlier, the saga calls him "the wisest man in Iceland, not counting those who were prescient" (chapter 114).

## The daughter and the memory

Snorri's daughter, Þuríðr, was one of the informants of Ari Þorgilsson, who took part in the first version of the *Landnámabók*. This is how family memories may have reached surviving texts.

## What we do not know

The sagas were written centuries after the events. Snorri is described consistently, as shrewd, far-sighted and vengeful, but each saga uses him for its own ends, and there is no other source to check against.

## In short

Snorri buys Helgafell, builds the church, swaps lands with Guðrún and arbitrates disputes at the Althing. What the sagas tell of him combines shrewdness and political weight, and that is what makes him recognizable in all three.
