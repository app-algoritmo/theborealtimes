Few Christmas tales have less Christmas and more fright than this one. It is from the collection Asbjørnsen and Moe published in Norway in 1852, and Dasent's English translation of 1859 helped spread it around the world. The fun lies in a question the trolls ask the following year.

## Halvor's house

A man from Finnmark is taking an enormous white bear to the king of Denmark. Near Christmas Eve he reaches the Dovrefjell and asks for shelter from Halvor, a farmer who lives in a cottage. Halvor refuses at first: every Christmas Eve the trolls visit the house, and the family has to leave and give the house over to them. The stranger says the house will do: the bear will lie under the stove and he will sleep in the side room. Halvor's family leaves, after laying a full feast for the trolls.

## The sausage

The trolls arrive, big and small, with different tails and noses. While they eat, a little troll notices the bear. He puts a piece of sausage on a fork and holds it out to the animal's nose, asking: "Pussy, will you have some sausage?" The bear rises, growls and chases them all until it has put them out.

## The next year's question

On Christmas Eve afternoon of the following year, Halvor is cutting wood when a troll calls from the forest and asks whether he still has that big cat. Halvor says yes, and that she now has seven kittens, far bigger and fiercer than she is. "Then we shall never come to see you again", says the troll, and the trolls never again ate Halvor's Christmas porridge on the Dovrefjell.

## The type of tale

The tale belongs to type 1161 of the Aarne–Thompson–Uther catalogue, "The Bear Trainer and His Cat". It is a migratory legend: a man with a tame bear spends the night in a house or mill haunted by trolls, goblins or water spirits, the bear drives them out, and a year later the creature asks about the "cat". The Norwegian version is only one of them.

## In short

A man with a white bear spends Christmas Eve in Halvor's house, the trolls try to tease the "cat" with a sausage, and the bear drives them out. The next year, the threat of seven kittens is enough to keep them away for good.

## Terms

The *Dovrefjell* is a mountain plateau in Norway. *Finnmark* is the far north of the country. A *tale type* is a pattern repeated in many places, catalogued by folklorists.

## What remains uncertain

Who first told the tale is unknown, and the version of Asbjørnsen and Moe is only the best known, not necessarily the oldest. The text does not explain why the trolls take over the house every year.
