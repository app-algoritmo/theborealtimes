The Trundholm sun chariot is one of the best-known objects of the Nordic Bronze Age. It is not from the Viking Age but from long before: it is about 3,400 years old. It appears here only as background to Nordic ideas about the sun.

## The find

It was found in 1902, in a peat bog at Trundholm, in Odsherred, in the northwest of Zealand, Denmark, with no other objects beside it. In 1998 archaeologists and metal detectorists returned to the site and recovered 21 more parts, including wheels and spokes.

## What it is

It is a bronze horse standing on a rod supported by four wheels, pulling a disc supported by two wheels. All the wheels have four spokes. The whole is about 54 cm long, 35 cm high and 29 cm wide, and the disc is about 25 cm in diameter. It was cast by the lost-wax technique, and a thin sheet of gold covers one side of the disc. The object is dated to about 1400 BC, Montelius period II, between 1500 and 1300 BC. Decorative details suggest Danubian influence, but it was made locally.

## How it is interpreted

The two sides of the disc are thought to represent the sun crossing the sky by day, with the gilded face, and returning by night, with the dark face. The chariot may have been pulled in rituals to show this movement. Christoph Sommerfeld proposed that the disc encodes the 19-year Metonic cycle, but this is a debated proposal. Two similar discs have been found in Ireland, the Lattoon one and the Cooke one. The link with the horse Skinfaxi, from Nordic myth, is only a suggested possibility.

## What is not known

It is not known how the parts were arranged or why they were deposited in the bog. The object is in the National Museum of Denmark, in Copenhagen, and appears on the 1,000-krone note of the 2009 series.
