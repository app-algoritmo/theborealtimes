Margaret Valdemarsdatter was born in March 1353 at Søborg Castle in Denmark, the youngest of the six children of King Valdemar IV and Helvig of Schleswig. The wedding to King Haakon VI of Norway took place in Copenhagen on 9 April 1363, when she was about ten. She was queen consort of Norway from 1363 to 1380 and of Sweden in 1363–1364, until the Swedish nobles deposed Haakon and elected Albert of Mecklenburg.

## Her son Olaf and the regency

Her son Olaf was born in 1370. After Valdemar's death in 1375, she secured his election as king of Denmark. Olaf succeeded Haakon in Norway in 1380 and died suddenly in 1387, aged 16. Margaret governed in his name: she was regent of Denmark from 3 May 1376 to 3 August 1387 and was chosen regent of Norway and Denmark in 1388. She recovered Schleswig under the Compact of Nyborg of 1386.

## Sweden and the battle of Åsle

Swedish nobles invited her to remove King Albert. In March 1388, at Dalaborg Castle, they accepted her conditions and called her "Sovereign Lady and Ruler". The decisive battle was on 24 February 1389, at Åsle, near Falköping. Her commander Henrik Parow was killed, but her forces won. Albert was released under the Compact of Lindholm (1395) on a promise to pay a ransom, and did not pay. The Hanseatic League surrendered Stockholm to her in September 1398.

## The Kalmar Union

In 1389 she proclaimed her great-nephew Bogislav king of Norway; she adopted him, and he became Erik of Pomerania. Erik was crowned king of Denmark, Norway and Sweden on 17 June 1397. The Act of Union was never completed, but the union of Denmark and Norway lasted until 1814. Even after Erik came of age in 1401, Margaret remained the effective ruler.

## Death and memory

Contemporaries called her "Lady King", and the historian Knut Gjerset called her "the first great ruling queen in European history". She died on 28 October 1412, aged 59, aboard a ship in Flensburg harbour. Her sarcophagus, made by Johannes Junge in 1423, stands behind the high altar of Roskilde Cathedral.
