When Thor's treasures are mentioned, the hammer Mjölnir comes first. The Prose Edda reminds us, though, that the god has two others: the belt Megingjörð and the iron gloves Járngreipr. They are discreet objects, but the sources give them a precise role.

## The belt of strength

The name Megingjörð means "power belt": megin is strength, gjörð is belt. According to Snorri Sturluson, when Thor tightens it his divine strength grows, and the source sums up the effect as doubling the strength he already has. In the Gylfaginning, right after the Prologue, Thor "girded himself with his belt of strength, and his divine strength grew".

The belt also appears in older poetry. In the Þórsdrápa, a 10th-century poem by Eilífr Goðrúnarson, made in the service of Jarl Hákon Sigurðarson, stanza 7 refers to the belt. The source translates the phrase the poet uses for Thor as "he who benefits from the mighty belt".

## The iron gloves

Snorri joins the belt to a third object: the iron gloves Járngreipr, whose name means "iron grips". By the text of the Gylfaginning, Thor needs them to hold the handle of the hammer. That is why the trio, hammer, belt and gloves, appears together as the god's three main possessions.

## What the sources do not say

Almost nothing is known about the origin of the belt and the gloves: in the sources I consulted there is no account of who made them, how Thor got them or what the belt was made of. The image of a wide leather belt with a buckle is a modern imagining, not an ancient description.

## In short

Megingjörð and Járngreipr show that Thor's strength, in the sources, is not only a natural gift: it comes with equipment. To use the gloves and catch the hammer, play [Thor's iron gloves](/game-thor-s-iron-gloves/).
