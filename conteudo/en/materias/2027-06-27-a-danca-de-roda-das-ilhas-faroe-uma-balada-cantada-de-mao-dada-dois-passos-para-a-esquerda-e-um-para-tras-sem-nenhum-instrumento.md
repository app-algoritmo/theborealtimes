In the Faroe Islands, the national dance is a ring. People form a circle, holding hands, and sing old ballads called *kvæði*. Each person's right hand passes a little over the neighbour's left, and the feet make two steps to the left and one back.

## Who sings and who answers

The one who leads the song is the *skipari*, the leader. He sings the verses, and everyone else joins in the refrain. There are no instruments. The only sound besides the voices is the stamping of feet on the floor, which keeps the beat. The ballads carry, from generation to generation, verses about the kings and heroes of the past.

## Three ways to dance

The most common variant is the *stígingarstev*, with a six-step pattern per bar. There are also the *trokingarstev* and the *bandadansur*, the ribbon dance. The dance is not just a show: anyone can join the ring, and latecomers simply add themselves to the circle.

## A season for dancing

The season runs roughly from September or October until Lent. On the bright summer nights, nobody dances. The exception is Ólavsøka, the national festival of 29 July, when dancing begins after midnight, after the singing, and goes on into the small hours. There are 15 associations gathered in the Sláið Ring, among them the Dansifelagið í Havn, founded in 1952.

## Who kept the ballads

One of the main written collections is Hammershaimb's *Færøsk Anthologi*, from 1891. Before books, they lived in memory, passed from one generation to the next inside the ring itself.
