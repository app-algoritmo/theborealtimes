Odin has a grey horse with eight legs, and almost everything known about it fits in a few sources. Its name is Sleipnir. He appears in the Poetic Edda, in the Prose Edda and on a painted stone from Gotland. His origin is the strangest part of the story.

## The horse and the wall

In the *Gylfaginning*, Snorri Sturluson tells how a builder offered the gods to raise a wall around Asgard in three winters. In return he wanted the sun, the moon and the hand of Freyja. The gods agreed on one condition: the wall had to be finished by the first day of summer. The builder had only the help of his stallion Svaðilfari, who hauled stones of a size that amazed the gods. When only a few days were left, the gods grew afraid and blamed Loki.

Loki took the shape of a mare and lured Svaðilfari away. The builder, who was a giant, did not finish the wall in time and Thor killed him. Some time later Loki gave birth to a grey foal with eight legs. That was Sleipnir, "the best of all horses", according to Snorri, and Loki gave him to Odin. The *Hyndluljóð* confirms the parentage in few words: Loki fathered the wolf on Angrboða, and Sleipnir he bore to Svaðilfari.

## What Sleipnir does

The *Grímnismál* says Sleipnir is the best of horses, just as Yggdrasil is the best of trees and Bifröst the best of bridges. Odin rides him on journeys between the worlds. In the *Baldrs draumar*, Odin rides Sleipnir to Hel to question a seeress about Baldr's dreams. In the *Gylfaginning*, after Baldr's death, the god Hermóðr borrows the horse and rides nine nights to the realm of Hel. At the gate, Sleipnir leaps over it.

## The Tjängvide stone

In 1844 a painted stone was found at Tjängvide, in Gotland, showing a rider on an eight-legged horse before a figure who receives him. It is now in the Swedish Museum of National Antiquities and is usually dated to about 700 to 900. Many scholars link it to Odin and Sleipnir, but the stone carries no name, and that link is an interpretation.

## What is hypothesis

Why eight legs? The written sources do not explain. Some scholars propose that the eight legs recall the four men who carry a coffin, which would fit the horse's role on the road to Hel. It is a theory. No Norse text says it.

## In short

Sleipnir is born of a change of shape: Loki, as a mare, and the stallion Svaðilfari. He stays with Odin, carries him between the worlds and carries Hermóðr to Hel. The Tjängvide stone shows a similar horse, and the link to Sleipnir is a modern reading.
