Þorsteins saga Síðu-Hallssonar is an Icelandic saga of the 13th century about events of the early 1000s. It has reached us incomplete: the beginning is lost, and the end too.

## The hero sets out

Þorsteinn Síðu-Hallsson goes abroad and leaves his chieftaincy, the goðorð, in the hands of his friend Þórhaddr. Abroad he serves Jarl Sigurðr of Orkney and fights at the battle of Clontarf, in 1014.

## The return and the dispute

In Iceland, Þórhaddr mistreats his son-in-law, Haukr. When Þorsteinn returns, Haukr asks him for help, and Þorsteinn demands his chieftaincy back. Þórhaddr refuses, and Þorsteinn drives him out of the district by force.

## The dreams

Þórhaddr slanders Þorsteinn and has disturbing dreams. They say that he and his sons will be killed by Þorsteinn, and that Þorsteinn will be killed, later, by a mere thrall.

## The deaths

Þorsteinn kills the three sons of Þórhaddr. The end of the saga is lost, and it is assumed that he kills Þórhaddr himself in a second encounter. Þorsteinn's fate is told separately, in a þáttr, the Draumr Þorsteins Síðu-Hallssonar, the "Dream of Þorsteinn".

## In short

Þorsteinn's saga mixes an Irish battle, a dispute over a chieftaincy and the force of a dream. To row in time with the boat toward Orkney, play [Toward Orkney](/game-toward-orkney/).
