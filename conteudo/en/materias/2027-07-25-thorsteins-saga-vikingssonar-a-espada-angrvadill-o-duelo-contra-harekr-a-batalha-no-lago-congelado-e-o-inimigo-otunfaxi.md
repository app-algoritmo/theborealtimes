The *Þorsteins saga Víkingssonar*, "the saga of Þorsteinn, son of Víkingr", is one of the later *fornaldarsögur*. It tells the story of two generations and ends with the birth of Friðþjófr, the hero of the *Friðþjófs saga ins frœkna*, which the author probably knew. The text has many characters, and the summary below follows only the main threads.

## Víkingr and the sword Angrvaðill

The saga begins with the quarrel of two earls, Vífill and Véseti, who abduct the daughters of King Haloge and are banished. Vífill stays on the island of Vífilsey, where his son Víkingr is born. King Hring, of Sweden, has a daughter, Hunvör. The gigantic warrior Hárekr Járnhauss demands the kingdom and her hand and proposes a duel. Víkingr agrees to face him, and Vífill gives him the sword Angrvaðill, a treasure of Kol Kroppinbakr. Víkingr kills Hárekr.

On an expedition, Víkingr drinks on an island from a woman, Sólbjört, and falls ill with leprosy. He meets Hálfdan Úlfsson, and the two become foster-brothers. With the help of the dwarf Litr, Hálfdan gets Dís's horn, and Víkingr is healed. The brothers beat Jökull Járnhryggr. Víkingr marries Hunvör, and Hálfdan marries Ingibjörg. Later King Njörfi, of Norway, makes peace with them, and Víkingr becomes his earl.

## The sons' quarrel and the battle on the ice

Njörfi has nine sons, led by Jökull; Víkingr has nine, led by Þorsteinn. In a ball game, Þórir kills Óláfr, Njörfi's son. Víkingr refuses to protect Þórir, but gives Angrvaðill to Þorsteinn and sends the brothers to an island in a lake. Jökull gathers men and two strangers, Gautan and Ógautan. Ógautan opens a magic bag that brings a storm and freezes the lake, letting Jökull's men cross it.

Þorsteinn dreams of animals attacking and sees an omen. In the battle, most of both families die. Þorsteinn kills Gautan with Angrvaðill, and Ógautan, by mistake, beheads Finnr, Þorsteinn's brother who looked like him. Víkingr finds the surviving sons at night and hides them in an underground house. The saga says that when he arms himself, Víkingr's age seems to fall away.

## Þorsteinn's long road

Víkingr sends Þorsteinn and Þórir to Hálfdan, in Vágar, warning of a chasm and of robbers. Þórir kills Sámr, and the brothers kill Fullafli and the dog Gramr. Þorsteinn wins a duel to get a farmer's daughter, Þóra, for Þórir. Into Þorsteinn's life comes Ingibjörg, daughter of King Skati, of Sogn, who was cursed and goes about in the shape of a creature, Skellinefja. She saves him from the sea and heals him in exchange for a promise of marriage, revealing herself. Þorsteinn beats Bele, whom he makes a foster-brother, and marries Ingibjörg. The two raid, and Þorsteinn kills the viking Úfi with Angrvaðill.

## Ötunfaxi and the end

The viking Ötunfaxi wants to avenge Úfi. Þorsteinn receives from the dwarf Sindri a belt knife and advice, and from Brenner, Víkingr's uncle, an iron club. In the battle, the fleets exchange stones and arrows, and Þorsteinn kills Ötunfaxi with the club and the knife. He keeps the ship *Ellide*. Later Jökull, the One-Handed, captures him, but Sindri frees him. Peace is made at Limfjord: Jökull regains his kingdom and keeps Þorsteinn's upland lands, as compensation for the hand he lost. Þorsteinn has a son, Friðþjófr, and a daughter, Véfreyja, who inherits the sword.

## What is uncertain

Scholars compare some motifs in the saga to Icelandic texts of the late fourteenth century, but the exact date is uncertain. The saga is tied to that of Friðþjófr, which shows that authors knew other stories. The saga ends by calling itself "most amusing".
