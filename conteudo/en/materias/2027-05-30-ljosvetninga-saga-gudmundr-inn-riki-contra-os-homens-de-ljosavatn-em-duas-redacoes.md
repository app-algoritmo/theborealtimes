Ljósvetninga saga is an Icelandic saga of the 13th century about the north of Iceland, around Ljósavatn. The figure who appears most in it is Guðmundr inn ríki Eyjólfsson, "the mighty", a feared chieftain, in conflict with the Ljósvetningar, the "men of Ljósavatn".

## Two redactions

The text has reached us in two redactions, called A and C. Redaction A is in manuscript AM 561 4to, of about 1400, and C in manuscript AM 162 c fol. Both tell the same story in quite different ways, and scholars argue over which of them is closer to the original.

## Who appears

Besides Guðmundr, the saga brings together figures who also appear in other narratives: Drauma-Finni, the man of dreams, Jarl Haakon and King Ólafr Haraldsson. The plot runs from a local dispute among chieftains of the north to episodes set in Norway.

## When and by whom

The date is uncertain. Some place it around 1220 and some in the middle of the 13th century. In 1953 Barði Guðmundsson proposed that the author was Þórðr Þorvarðsson, but this is only a hypothesis. Another debate is whether the text comes from an oral tradition, "freeprose", or from desk work, "bookprose".

## Edition and translation

The reference edition in Icelandic is that of Björn Sigfússon, from 1940. In English the saga was translated by Andersson and Miller in 1989, in the same volume as Valla-Ljóts saga.
