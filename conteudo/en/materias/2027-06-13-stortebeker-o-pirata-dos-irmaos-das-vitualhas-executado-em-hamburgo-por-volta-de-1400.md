Klaus Störtebeker was born around 1360 and, according to Wikipedia, died supposedly on 20 October 1401 in Hamburg. The article writes "supposedly": records of 1400 about graves for 30 Victual Brothers suggest the date may be 1400.

## The Victual Brothers

They were privateers in the war between Sweden and Denmark. They had a base in Visby, on the island of Gotland, from which they were expelled around 1398. They were also called *Likedeelers*, those who share equally.

## The chase

In August 1400, a Hanseatic fleet, with Hermann Langhe and Nikolaus Schoke, sailed to Heligoland. The ship *Bunte Kuh* was finished only in 1401, which makes it hard to support the version that puts it in the 1400 hunt. The source records this contradiction and does not resolve it.

## The legends

The best known says Störtebeker asked that the men his beheaded body walked past be freed, and that he walked past eleven of them before falling. Another tells of a golden chain that would go once around Hamburg. Another, of masts of gold, silver and copper, said to be meant for St Catherine's church. The 73 companions executed are legend, and the records point to about 30.

## The skull

The skull, displayed from 1922, was stolen in 2010 and recovered in 2011. The most common etching of Störtebeker actually shows the jester Kunz von der Rosen.
