*Assipattle and the Stoor Worm* is a tale from Orkney, in northern Scotland, about a young man who defeats a sea monster. The source consulted for this piece describes the plot, who recorded it and the debate among scholars about its origins. This piece separates what the tale narrates from what is interpretation.

## Who recorded the tale

The version we know was preserved by Walter Traill Dennison and published in *The Scottish Antiquary* in 1891. A collection by Douglas, of about 1893 or 1896, also carries the tale. Marwick made a retelling.

## The plot

Assipattle is the seventh son, not necessarily the youngest. In the story appear Princess Gem-de-lovely, a sorcerer, the sword Sickersnapper and the horse Teetgong. Assipattle sets the monster's liver alight with a burning peat. The worm writhes and dies.

## The origin of the islands

As it dies, the monster gives rise to places on the map. The teeth become the islands of Orkney, Shetland and the Faroes. The tongue becomes the Baltic Sea, and the body becomes Iceland. That is why the tale is read as an explanation of the birth of lands.

## How the tale links the monster to the map

The tale works as a map in the form of a story: each part of the monster corresponds to a place in the North Atlantic or the Baltic. The teeth are northern islands, the tongue is a sea and the whole body is a large island. This piece does not claim that this is ancient geographical knowledge: it is only the logic of the tale.

## The hero

Assipattle is the seventh son, and the source takes care to say that he is not necessarily the youngest. The remark matters because hero tales usually favour the youngest, and here it is the number seven, not the age, that the source records.

## The debate about origins

The source records two opinions. Dasent published a version with a hero called Boots. Hartland considers it "purely Norse". Simpson considers it "almost pure *märchen*", that is, an ordinary fairy tale of a European type. The source does not decide between the two readings.

## Terms

*Stoor Worm* is the name of the great sea worm. *Orkney* is an archipelago in northern Scotland. *Peat* is compact plant soil used as fuel. *Märchen* is the German word for fairy tale. *Sickersnapper* is the name of the sword.

## What remains uncertain

The source does not mention the Greek hero Perseus or the serpent Jörmungandr: links of that kind are free, and this piece does not assert them. Whether the tale is of Norse origin or of a common European type is debated. The form in which we know it was recorded in the 19th century, and it is not known precisely how much of it is much older.
