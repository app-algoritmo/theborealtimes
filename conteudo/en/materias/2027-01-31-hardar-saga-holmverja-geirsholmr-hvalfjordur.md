*Harðar saga ok Hólmverja*, the "saga of Hǫrðr and the men of the islet", is one of the sagas of Icelanders. The source consulted for this piece describes the plot, the setting and the manuscripts that preserve it. This piece follows what the source records and leaves aside what it does not state.

## The setting and the manuscripts

The story is set in south-western Iceland, in the latter half of the 900s, that is, long before the time when the manuscripts were copied. The saga survives in medieval and later manuscripts. The main one is AM 556a 4to, from about 1475. There is also a fragment, AM 564a 4to, from about 1400. In addition, there are about 37 paper manuscripts. This piece does not claim a date when the saga was composed: only the surviving manuscripts are dated.

## The plot

The protagonist is Hǫrðr Grímkelsson. He joins the *Hólmverjar*, the "men of the islet". The story includes Helga Haraldsdóttir, who is bound to Hǫrðr, and the revenge of Torfi. Hǫrðr is declared an outlaw, and the group attacks and burns a farm. They take refuge in Geirshólmr, an islet in Hvalfjörður, and come to live there.

The farmers of the region unite against the group and lay a plot with Kjartan Kǫtluson. Hǫrðr and Geirr are executed. Helga and the sons flee by swimming to the shore. Later, Hǫrðr's sons, the Harðarsons, avenge their father.

## The place in the plot

The islet is the centre of the plot: the men take refuge on it, the farmers' plot is organised against the group living there, and from it Helga and the sons flee by swimming. Hvalfjörður is a fjord of south-western Iceland, the same region in which the source sets the whole story. Between the islet and the shore, therefore, pass the flight, the revenge and the end of Hǫrðr.

## Terms

*Saga of Icelanders* is a genre of prose narrative about the first settlers of Iceland and their descendants. An *outlaw* (*útlagi*) is someone who loses the protection of the law and can be killed without penalty. *Hólmverjar* means "men of the islet". *AM* refers to the Árni Magnússon collection, where the manuscripts are kept. *Geirshólmr* is the name of the islet, and *Hvalfjörður* is the fjord in which it lies, in south-western Iceland.

## What remains uncertain

The source does not give the saga's date of composition in this piece, and the relation of the manuscripts to an older text is not detailed. The source page itself carries a citation that seems to point to *Hávarðar saga*, which may be a citation error, and so this piece does not rely on it. What the saga tells of the men of the islet is literary narrative: there is no proof here that every event happened as it is written.
