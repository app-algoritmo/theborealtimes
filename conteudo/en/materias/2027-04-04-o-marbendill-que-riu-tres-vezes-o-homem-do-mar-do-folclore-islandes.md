In Iceland, the *marbendill* is a merman: a small being who lives in the water and appears only when a fisherman hooks him by chance. Tradition gives him two powers. He knows the future and what is hidden, and he laughs at people who do foolish things. This text gathers what I read about him, with attention to a famous tale by Jón Árnason.

## One name, several forms

The word also appears as *marmennill*, *marbendill* or *margmelli*, and is translated as "merman", "little merman" or "sea goblin". The name even enters the names of things: one kind of coral is called *marmendils-smíði*, "the marbendill's work", and a seaweed, *marmendils-þari*, "the marbendill's weed".

## Jón Árnason's tale

In the tale "Þá hló marbendill", a farmer catches a marbendill and takes him home. The creature laughs three times. He laughs when the farmer's wife greets him, because she is unfaithful. He laughs when the farmer hits the dog, because the dog truly loves him. And he laughs when the farmer trips over a tuft of grass, because the grass hides a treasure. The farmer asks for the reasons, and the marbendill explains them in exchange for his freedom. The man lets him go, and everything he said proves true.

## An older saga

The *Hálfs saga ok Hálfsrekka*, from the fourteenth century, has a similar episode. In chapter 7, "Frá spám marmennils", a father and son catch a marmennill and bring him to King Hjörleifr. The creature stays silent at court, except for one fit of laughter. When he returns to the sea, he makes several prophecies in verse. The *Landnámabók*, the book of the settlers, also mentions a marmennill, according to the page I consulted, but without the details I read in this saga.

## An expression that stayed

The laughter of the tale became so well known that the expression *þá hló marbendill*, "then the marbendill laughed", is used in Iceland for a sudden, spiteful burst of laughter, the kind that seems to know more than it says.

## Other beings that warn

The page notes that other beings also give warnings, such as the sea monsters *hafstramba* and *margýgr*, which appear before storms and shipwrecks, and that Odin himself, in a text called *Böglunda sögur*, foretells a great battle and a death.

## In short

The marbendill is the merman who knows what is hidden. In Jón Árnason's tale he laughs three times and pays for his freedom with three truths. In a fourteenth-century saga, he makes prophecies in verse.

## Terms

*Marbendill* and *marmennill* are forms of the same name, "merman". *Landnámabók* is the book of the settlers of Iceland.

## What remains uncertain

I did not see the original text of the tale or of the sagas, only descriptions on reference pages, so I treat the details as reported by them. The relation between the marbendill and other sea beings, such as the selkie or the nix, is only a comparison made by the page consulted.
