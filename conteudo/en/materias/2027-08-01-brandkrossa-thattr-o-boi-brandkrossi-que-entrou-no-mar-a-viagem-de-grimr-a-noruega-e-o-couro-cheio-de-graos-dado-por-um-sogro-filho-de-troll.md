The *Brandkrossa þáttr* is a short Icelandic tale that became linked to the *Droplaugarsona saga*, the saga of Droplaug's sons, and works as its prologue. It survives only in paper manuscripts, the oldest from the seventeenth century, all derived from a lost manuscript.

## The tale

The title comes from the name of an ox, Brandkrossi, who belonged to Grímr, an ancestor of Droplaug's sons. The story falls into two parts, and the second is the more fantastic.

## The ox that went to sea

One day, the ox Brandkrossi has a fit of rage and swims into the sea. Grímr then travels to Norway.

## Geitir and his daughter

There, Grímr meets Geitir, a man whose father was a troll. Grímr marries Geitir's daughter, Droplaug. Then it is revealed that it was Geitir who made Brandkrossi disappear. Geitir sends Grímr off with many gifts, among them Brandkrossi's hide filled with grain.

## What remains uncertain

The *Droplaugarsona saga* follows the descendants of Helgi Droplaugarson, and this tale looks to the forebears of his enemy, Helgi Ásbjarnarson. The author seems to have used a version of the *Landnámabók* and not to have known the *Hrafnkels saga*, and there is a similar episode in the *Fljótsdæla saga*. The encounter of a traveller with a giant or son of a troll in Norway recalls episodes of the *Kjalnesinga saga* and the *Hálfdanar þáttr svarta*. The date of composition is not known.
