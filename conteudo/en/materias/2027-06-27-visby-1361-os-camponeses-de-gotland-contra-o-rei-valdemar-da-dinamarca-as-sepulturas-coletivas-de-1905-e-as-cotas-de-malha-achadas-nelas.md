On 27 July 1361, outside the town of Visby, on the Swedish island of Gotland, a battle took place that the Swedes call Korsbetningen. On one side were King Valdemar IV of Denmark and a Danish and German force, largely mercenaries. On the other, the free farmers of Gotland, the Gutes, led by a chief whose name history did not keep, probably a minor noble.

## The landing

Valdemar had landed on the west coast of the island on 22 July. With somewhere between 2,000 and 2,500 men, he marched towards Visby. The Gutes faced him in open country, outside the town walls. The main source estimates that about 1,800 farmers died, while Danish losses are not known with certainty. The victory went to the Danes.

## What the ground kept

The archaeological interest dates from 1905. Excavations led by Oscar Wilhelm Wennersten and the master builder Nils Pettersson found the first mass grave, and then five, outside the walls. About 2,000 bodies were taken up. Many had been buried in their armour: the Gutes commonly wore a mail shirt or a coat of plates over the torso, and many had only a mail coif on the head. The helmets may have been stripped after the battle. The Danish and German soldiers, by contrast, wore transitional armour.

## The town that paid

Visby surrendered and paid a large ransom to Valdemar to avoid being sacked, although the details are unclear. Even so, the Danes plundered several churches and monasteries. Valdemar appointed sheriffs and sailed away. About a year later he added "King of Gotland" to his titles. The island was disputed until 1376, when Queen Margaret claimed it for Denmark.

## Remembered today

Since 2011, the battle has been reenacted outside the wall of Visby. Every Medieval Week, Valdemar's entrance and the ransoming of the town are reenacted.
