On 23 December, Iceland celebrates Þorláksmessa, the Mass of Þorlákur. In the Faroe Islands the day is called *Tollaksmessa*. It is the feast of Iceland's most popular saint and, today, almost a Christmas ritual.

## The man

Þorlákur Þórhallsson was born in 1133 at Hlíðarendi. He studied in Paris, around 1153 to 1159, and in Lincoln. He founded the Þykkvabær monastery in 1168 and was elected bishop of Skálholt in 1174. Eysteinn Erlendsson, archbishop of Niðaróss, consecrated him in 1178. He died on 23 December 1193.

## From bishop to saint

In 1198, after a proposal by Páll Jónsson at the Althing, his relics were translated, which marked the start of informal veneration. John Paul II canonized him on 14 January 1984 and declared him patron saint of Iceland.

## The skate

According to the Iceland Monitor (2018), it is customary to eat *skata*, fermented skate, at midday, and shops stay open until 11 pm for the last shopping. Smoked lamb is also common. The last of the Yule Lads, Kertasníkir, arrives that night.

Atlas Obscura links the dish to the fast before Christmas. It says skate can be found all year, that the ammonia smell comes from uric acid, and that the fish is fermented for a few weeks and then steamed or boiled, served with buttered rye bread, potatoes and Brennivín.

## What the sources do not say

The sources consulted do not explain how skate came to be tied to the saint. The text stays with what they record.
