Ingigerðr, or Ingegerd, was born around the year 1001, daughter of the Swedish king Olof Skötkonung and Estrid of the Obotrites. She married a prince of Kievan Rus and died in 1050. Her life links Sweden, Norway and the Slavic-speaking world.

## The promise her father broke

According to the sources, in 1015 King Olaf II of Norway proposed a royal alliance. In 1016, noblemen of both countries tried to arrange his marriage to Ingigerðr. Olof Skötkonung agreed at first, then went back on it and promised Olaf his other daughter, Astrid.

## The marriage to Yaroslav

Olof then arranged her marriage to Yaroslav I the Wise of Novgorod, a trade partner of Sweden. The wedding was in 1019. In Kiev, she came to be called Irene, in the Greek manner.

## The gift of Ladoga

According to several sagas, Ingigerðr received Ladoga and neighbouring lands as a wedding gift. These lands later became known as Ingria, a name that may be a corruption of hers. She put the jarl Ragnvald Ulfsson, her father's cousin, in charge in her place.

## The children

Ingigerðr and Yaroslav had six sons and four daughters. Three daughters became queens: Anne, of France, Anastasia, of Hungary, and Elisiv, of Norway. The article lists among the possible sons Agatha, Vladimir of Novgorod, Iziaslav I, Sviatoslav II, Vsevolod I and Igor Yaroslavich.

## The death and the debate on Saint Anna

Ingigerðr died on 10 February 1050. Sources differ on whether she was buried in Saint Sophia's Cathedral in Kiev or in Novgorod. Tradition identifies her with Saint Anna of Novgorod, but scholars dispute this. A. V. Nazarenko thinks it more likely that Saint Anna was an earlier wife of Yaroslav. Maria Korogodina and Aleksey Sirenov question whether the name is historical, since it appears only in records from the early sixteenth century. The Russian Primary Chronicle credits Yaroslav with founding Saint Sophia's Cathedral in Kiev (1037).

## In short

Ingigerðr's life shows how Norse and Slavic royal families were tied by marriage and how a name, Ingria, can stay on a map because of a bride. What is said of the saint is less certain than what is said of the princess.
