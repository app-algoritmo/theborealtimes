The Battle of Nesjar was a naval engagement fought on 25 March 1016, Palm Sunday, in Norway, between Olaf Haraldsson, the future saint, and Sveinn Hákonarson. This piece uses two sources, two entries of an encyclopaedia in English, and separates what they state from what they leave open.

## The two sides

Sveinn Hákonarson, also written Sweyn, ruled as co-ruler of Norway from 1000 until about 1015. Olaf arrived in Norway, according to the second source, in 1015, although other dates, 1014 and 1016, also circulate. At Nesjar, Sveinn had the backing of Erling Skjalgsson and of farmer chieftains. The two sides met in ships, and the battle ended in Olaf's favour.

## The site

Tradition places Nesjar at the western end of the Oslofjord, in the Langesundfjorden region, near Brunlanes, in Larvik. At Helgeroa there is today the Nesjar Monument, which recalls the fight. The first source says the number of combatants is unknown.

## The poem

The source recalls that the poet Sigvatr Þórðarson commemorated the victory in the *Nesjavísur*, verses on the battle. The best-known narrative of Nesjar comes from later sources such as the *Heimskringla* and the *Fagrskinna*, which appear as references in the entries.

## After the battle

According to the first source, none of the main leaders died at Nesjar. Sveinn went to Sweden and, according to the second source, died of an illness around 1016. Erling Skjalgsson, his ally, was killed at Boknafjorden in 1028, and Olaf fell at Stiklestad two years later.

## Timeline

The dates the two sources give, in order, are these: from 1000 until about 1015, Sveinn reigns as co-ruler. In 1015, according to the second source, Olaf arrives in Norway. On 25 March 1016 the Battle of Nesjar takes place. Around 1016, Sveinn dies of illness in Sweden. In 1028, Erling Skjalgsson is killed at Boknafjorden. Two years later comes Stiklestad.

## Terms

A *co-ruler* is someone who governs jointly with another. A *naval battle* is a fight in ships. A *skald* is a Norse poet, such as Sigvatr. *Palm Sunday* is the Sunday before Easter. A *farmer chieftain* is a local leader of free farmers, here on the side of Sveinn.

## What remains uncertain

The number of men and ships is not known. The exact site is a matter of tradition, not of proof. The date of Olaf's arrival varies between 1014, 1015 and 1016, and the end of Sveinn, dead of illness in Sweden, is given only with the approximate date of 1016. The *Heimskringla* and the *Fagrskinna* were written after the event. The sources used here are encyclopaedia entries and do not allow the manoeuvres of the battle to be reconstructed.
