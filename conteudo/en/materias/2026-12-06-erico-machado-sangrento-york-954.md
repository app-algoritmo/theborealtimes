Eric "Bloodaxe" appears in the sagas as a cruel and violent king, but the sources closest to his time tell a drier and more political story. What is known with certainty is that he was king of Norway for a short time, that he later ruled York in northern England, and that he lost that throne twice.

## From Norway to exile

Eric was a son of Harald Fairhair. The sources disagree about the length of his reign: some place it between 931 and 933, others between 932 and 934. According to the *Heimskringla*, written in the 13th century, his half-brother Haakon returned from England with the support of Norwegian nobles, among them Sigurd, earl of Lade, and Eric had to flee to the British Isles. Later versions say he had a base in Orkney, but an older source, the *Fagrskinna*, does not have him landing there.

## The first reign in York

The *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* gives the main facts. In 947 King Eadred of Wessex met Archbishop Wulfstan and the Northumbrian nobles at Tanshelf, where they swore obedience. But according to manuscript D, the Northumbrians broke their oaths, and in 948 "they had taken Eric for their king". Eadred responded by raiding the north, burning the minster at Ripon and forcing the Northumbrians to renounce Eric and pay compensation. After that another king of Norse descent, Amlaíb Cuarán, took the throne for a while.

## The second reign and the fall

Text E of the *Chronicle* says that in 952 the Northumbrians drove out Amlaíb and accepted Eric again. In the same year Archbishop Wulfstan was arrested and tried, possibly because of this change. In 954, manuscripts D and E agree on one point: the Northumbrians expelled Eric once more. It was the end of the independent Scandinavian kingdom at York. Soon afterwards the north came to be governed by earls under the king of Wessex.

## How he died

Here the sources part ways. Roger of Wendover, in the early 13th century, says that Eric was treacherously killed by an earl called Maccus at Stainmore, after being betrayed by Earl Osulf, together with his son Haeric and his brother Ragnald. The *Fagrskinna* and the *Heimskringla*, for their part, say he died in battle at an unnamed place in England, alongside five other kings. Other Norwegian sources speak of an expedition to Spain, and one scholar proposed that "Span-" was a scribal error for "Stan-". Most modern researchers prefer to think of an assassination during exile. The Rey Cross near Stainmore, which has been linked to him, is probably a 10th-century boundary marker. No grave has been found.

## The Bloodaxe of the sagas

The nickname "Bloodaxe" (*blóðøx*) appears in texts written after Eric's lifetime. In *Egil's saga*, the poet Egill Skallagrímsson is captured by him at York and saves his life by composing a poem in his praise. That scene is famous, but it comes from a saga written in the 13th century, not from a record of the time.

## What remains

Eric shows how fragile the power of Scandinavian kings in England was. They depended on the support of local nobles, of the Church and on the constant threat of the king of Wessex. When they lost one of these supports, they lost the throne.
