The *Dámusta saga*, also called *Dámusta saga ok Jóns* or *Saga spekingsins Dámusta í Gricklandi*, is an Icelandic chivalric saga. The work stands out for the inventive way it engages with the traditions of Continental romance, a subject studied by Margaret Schlauch in 1937.

## The knight and the princess

Dámusti is a pious knight at the court of King Katalaktus of Greece (*Grikkland*). The king's daughter, Gratiana, marries King Jón of Smáland. Out of jealousy, Dámusti kills Jón. Gratiana falls gravely ill.

## The Virgin and the giant

Dámusti repents, and the Virgin Mary appears to him. She orders him to arm himself and ride to the church where Gratiana is buried. There he defeats the giant Alheimr, who reveals that he faked Gratiana's death in order to marry her. Alheimr gives him a potion that revives the young woman.

## The forgiveness

Katalaktus forgives Dámusti, and the knight marries Gratiana. When their son is old enough to rule, Dámusti and Gratiana become hermits.

## Manuscripts

The date of composition is not given in the sources. The oldest manuscript is AM 557 4°, of the fifteenth century, with three leaves, and the most recent is of the nineteenth century. There are copies in Copenhagen (AM 557 4°, AM 588e 4°, Rask 32), in London, in Reykjavík (for example JS 27 fol., from about 1670), in Trondheim, in Lund, in Stockholm and in the Beinecke Library at Yale, which shows the reach of the work.

## Editions

J. Tan-Haverhorst edited the saga in 1939 from JS 27 fol., together with *Þjalar Jóns saga*. In 1940, Dag Strömbäck published a facsimile of folios 38r to 40v of AM 557 4°.

To catch the drops of the potion that revives Gratiana, avoiding the giant's stones, play [Alheimr's potion](/game-alheimr-s-potion/).
