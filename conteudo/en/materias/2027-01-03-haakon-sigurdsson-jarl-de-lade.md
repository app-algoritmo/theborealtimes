Haakon Sigurdsson, also called Earl Haakon, was born around 937 and was lord of northern Norway in the days of Harald Bluetooth and of Olaf Tryggvason. According to the source consulted, he was the son of Sigurd Haakonsson, Earl of Lade, in Trøndelag and Hålogaland, and of Bergljot Toresdatter.

## The house of Lade

Adam of Bremen says the family descended from giants, from "Ivar". The sagas give another origin: from Sæming, son of Odin. When his father was killed, in 961, by men of Harald Greycloak, Haakon became earl. Around 971 he arranged the death of Greycloak himself, with the connivance of Harald Bluetooth.

## Vassal and independent

Haakon was a vassal of Harald Bluetooth, but in practice ruled on his own. He attacked Götaland and killed Earl Ottar. Around 973 and 974 he helped Harald against Emperor Otto II, and afterwards stopped paying tribute. About 975 he broke with Denmark when Harald pressed for Christianity. Haakon had already been baptised, and ordered the clergy ashore when the wind turned. In 986, the Danish fleet of the Jomsvikings was defeated at Hjörungavágr. The article does not say what his role was there.

## Olaf arrives

When Olaf Tryggvason appeared, Haakon quarrelled with the Trønders, the people of Trøndelag. According to the sources, he was accused of abuses, but the article presents this as claims from the sagas, not as fact. Haakon died around 995, hiding in a pigsty at Rimul, in Melhus, killed by Tormod Kark, a slave and friend. The traditional place is called Jarlshola, but that is only a guess at the hiding place. Haakon's sons, Eirik and Sveinn, fled to Sweden, to Olof Skötkonung, and according to the article on Olaf took part at Svolder.

## Memory

There are runes in Sweden, called the Hakon Jarl runestones, that "may refer" to him. Hallfreðr's poem about Haakon is lost. In modern times, the Dane Adam Oehlenschläger wrote the tragedy *Hakon Jarl*, in 1805, Smetana composed a symphonic poem, and in Trøndelag there is the play *Håkon og Kark*, at the Korsvikaspillet.

## How to read the sources

Everything known about Haakon comes from texts written after his death, and the article treats the accusations of abuse as claims made by the sources, not as proven facts. The few points where the sources overlap, such as the death at the hands of the slave Kark, are the most secure.

## What remains uncertain

The sources on Haakon are late, and neither the exact birth date nor the precise location of the hiding place is known. What is on firmer ground: that he ruled for a long time, that he opposed Olaf and that a slave killed him.
