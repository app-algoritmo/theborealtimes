Kári Sölmundarson is, in Njáls saga, the character the story does not let die. He was a Hebridean viking and mercenary of the late tenth and early eleventh century, son of Sölmundr and grandson of Þorbjörn, "the Jarl's Champion", an Icelander exiled for homicide before the Althing existed. Where he was born is unknown; perhaps Iceland. He served as a hirdman of Sigurd the Stout, jarl of Orkney.

## Two sons of Njáll saved at sea

Around the 990s, Kári saved Helgi and Grímr, sons of Njáll, who were trading in the British Isles and were attacked by the vikings Snækólfr and Grjótgarðr Mörðsson. He took the two to Orkney, where they fought in the jarl's campaigns in Scotland. Later he saved them again, when Hákon, jarl of Norway, arrested them for helping Þráinn Sigfússon escape. He returned with them to Iceland, bought land at Dyrhólmar, settled at Bergþórshvoll, married Helga, Njáll's daughter, and became a close friend of Skarphéðinn.

## The fire

Around 1010, Þráinn's allies, led by Flosi Þórðarson, set fire to Bergþórshvoll with everyone inside. Kári escaped under cover of smoke; according to one summary, running along a beam of the house. Helgi and Skarphéðinn died. Njáll, his wife Bergþóra and Kári's son Þórðr refused Flosi's offer and stayed.

## Revenge and the journey

Kári gathered support and brought a case against the Burners. The legal clash nearly turned into civil war, until Snorri goði separated the sides. The culprits were sentenced to three years of exile, but Kári attacked them on their way home and pursued the survivors abroad, as far as Orkney and Wales. In the jarl Sigurd's hall he killed Gunnarr Lambason, who was mocking the story of the burning. During the battle of Clontarf, in 1014, Kári stayed in Caithness. His wife Helga died in this period in Scotland.

## The peace

Flosi returned to Iceland from a pilgrimage to Rome. Kári followed and was shipwrecked near his home. He tested Flosi's nobility by asking for help, and the two made a final peace. Kári married Hildigunnr, Flosi's niece and the widow of Höskuldr Þráinsson. His sword was called Fjörsváfnir.
