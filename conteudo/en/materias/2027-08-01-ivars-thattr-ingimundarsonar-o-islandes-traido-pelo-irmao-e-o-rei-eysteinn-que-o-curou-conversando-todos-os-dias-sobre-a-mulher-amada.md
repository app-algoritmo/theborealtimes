The *Ívars þáttr Ingimundarsonar* is one of the most human tales in the *Morkinskinna*, the thirteenth-century compilation of Norwegian kings' sagas. In it, the centre is not a battle or a revenge, but a grief of love that a king decides to accompany.

## The Icelander at court

Ívarr is an Icelander who lives at the court of King Eysteinn in Norway, and the king is fond of him. Ívarr's brother, Þorfinnr, also travels to Norway, but he is jealous of him and soon returns to Iceland.

## The message not delivered

Before leaving, Þorfinnr hears a request from Ívarr: that he tell the woman Ívarr loves to wait for him. But Þorfinnr does not do so. He marries her himself.

## The sadness and the king's remedy

When Ívarr learns of the marriage, he is deeply shaken. The king notices his dejection and asks what is wrong. On learning, Eysteinn offers help so that Ívarr can marry the woman, and Ívarr explains that she is now his brother's wife. The king tries in several ways to lift his friend's sadness, without result.

Then Eysteinn proposes something simple: that the two talk about the woman every day, because pain can ease when it is shared. They do so, and Ívarr gradually recovers.

## What remains uncertain

The tale does not give the woman's name or any dates. Scholars discuss it as an example of the *þáttr* genre, the short tale inside a larger saga, and Ármann Jakobsson has questioned the use of that name for Icelandic tales. In 2011, an article in the medical journal *BMJ* presented it as an early example of care through conversation, and another recent study reads it in the frame of mental health at the Norwegian court. These modern readings are interpretations and do not say what the author meant.
