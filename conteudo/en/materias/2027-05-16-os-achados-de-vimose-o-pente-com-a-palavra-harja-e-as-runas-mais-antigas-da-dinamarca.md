At Vimose, on the Danish island of Funen, objects were found with some of the oldest datable inscriptions in Elder Futhark, written in early Proto-Norse or late Proto-Germanic, between the 2nd and 3rd centuries, in the time of the Roman Empire. They are short words on everyday objects: a comb, a buckle, a chape, a plane.

## The comb

The most famous is the comb, of about 160, considered the oldest datable runic inscription known. It says harja. The sources I consulted do not give the meaning, and the sense of the word is debated.

## The buckle and the chape

The buckle, of about 200, bears aadagasu and laasauwija, with tentative readings such as ansuz-a(n)dag-a(n)su and la-a[n]sau-wija. A scabbard chape, of about 250, says mariha and [.]ala makija, which is possibly read "Mari, the famous one, is the sword of Alla".

## The plane, the plate and the spearhead

The wooden plane, of about 300, is damaged and begins talijo gisai oj. A sheath plate bears awgns, which perhaps means "son or descendant of Awa". A spearhead bears [w]agni[ŋ]o.

## Deposits in a bog

Vimose, like Illerup and Thorsberg, is a site of deposits in bogs, in which weapons and objects were thrown into the water. Elmar Seebold's essay of 1994 deals with the linguistic interpretation of these archaic inscriptions.

## In short

The runes of Vimose do not tell myths: they are names, perhaps of owners or makers, and words we cannot yet read. To find the runes on the comb, play [The runes of the Vimose comb](/game-the-runes-of-the-vimose-comb/).
