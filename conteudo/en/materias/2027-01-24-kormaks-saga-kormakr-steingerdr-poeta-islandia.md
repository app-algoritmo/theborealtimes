## A poet and a bride

The saga is about Kormák Ögmundarson, an Icelandic poet of the 10th century. The woman he loves is Steingerðr Þórkelsdóttir, to whom he becomes betrothed. The article gives the plot a simple line, and the text is organised around it.

## The curse and the wedding

Because of a curse, Kormákr arrives too late for his own wedding, and Steingerðr marries another man. That is the turn of the story: the betrothal exists, but the wedding does not happen as the poet expected. The article does not say who cast the curse, and this piece does not invent it.

## Exile and death

After that, Kormákr follows King Harald Greycloak to Ireland. Later, in Scotland, he dies in a battle with a wizard. The article sums up the rest of the poet's life this way, in two short sentences.

## The verses

The saga preserves a significant number of poems attributed to Kormák, the *lausavísur*, many of them about his love for Steingerðr. In one set of stanzas he recalls how he met her, and these verses represent the style and content of his love poems. ## The manuscript and the date

The saga was probably written in the first part of the 13th century, and it is believed to be among the earliest composed, although it survives well. The only complete version is in the Icelandic manuscript *Möðruvallabók*, AM 132 fol.

## Terms

A *saga of Icelanders* is the genre of prose narratives about Icelandic families. A *lausavísa*, plural *lausavísur*, is a loose stanza attributed to a poet. *Möðruvallabók* is the manuscript that holds the only complete version. *AM 132 fol.* is its shelfmark in the Árni Magnússon collection.
