Haakon Sverresson, Haakon III, was born around 1183 and was king of Norway from 1202 to 1204. He was the son of Sverre, the future king, who was then a Faroese adventurer. P. A. Munch proposed that his mother was Astrid Roesdatter, daughter of Bishop Roe of the Faroe Islands, but later historians have not accepted the idea.

## After his father

Sverre died on 9 March 1202. Haakon was taken as chieftain by the Birkebeiners and acclaimed king at the Nidaros thing that spring. Sverre and Haakon led the Birkebeiner party in the civil wars. Haakon was not married.

## The winter of 1203

After Christmas 1203, Haakon fell ill after a bloodletting. He died on 1 January 1204. He was buried at Christ Church in Bergen, demolished in 1531. A memorial marks the place today.

## The suspicion about Margareta

His death was suspected to have been poisoning, and the suspicion fell on Margareta, Sverre's queen. She had a man undergo an ordeal to prove she was innocent. The man was badly burned, and that was taken as proof of her guilt. Margareta fled to Sweden. The poisoning is presented as a suspicion, not as an established fact.

## Guttorm and the succession

The successor was Guttorm Sigurdsson, Haakon's nephew, about four years old. Besides him, Inga Olafsdatter, of Varteig, said that her small son was Haakon's. The boy later became Haakon IV. In 1218, Inga went through an ordeal to support the claim.

## What can be said

The sources are Sverris saga and the Bagler sagas, both from the thirteenth century, written shortly after the events. They record the suspicion and the ordeal, but do not resolve it.
