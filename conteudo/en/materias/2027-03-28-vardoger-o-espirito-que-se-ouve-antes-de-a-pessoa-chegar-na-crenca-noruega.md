In Norwegian homes, tradition says, one sometimes hears the door creak and steps on the stairs, and nobody appears. Shortly afterwards, the person who was expected really arrives. The belief has a name: *vardøger*. For this text, I used an entry by a Norwegian encyclopedia author and the two books it lists.

## What a vardøger is

The Store norske leksikon defines the vardøger as "a spirit that announces and that is heard, more rarely seen, before the person it belongs to appears". The same text brings it close to a protective spirit, like the *fylgje*, and recalls that it can be understood as a kind of guardian angel. The word comes from Old Norse and may be related to "watcher" or "guardian". Other names are *vardyvle*, *vardyger* and *føreferd*.

## How it shows itself

According to the text, the presence is usually noticed through sounds: the creak of a door, noise on the stairs, footsteps. Sometimes one hears sounds characteristic of the person who owns the vardøger, such as a cough. The stories exist "from the whole country", and some people still believe them.

## Nordic relatives

The article cites similar beliefs in other countries. In Finland, the *etiäinen* is a double that does the same things the person will do later. In Iceland, the *fylgja* is the spirit of a dead person of the same family, which goes ahead of a descendant. In Ireland, the *fetch* is often tied to a warning of death or illness. In Sweden, the equivalent expression is something like "comes before they come".

## A belief that warns and draws near

What stands out in the article is that the vardøger does not frighten: it only anticipates. Whoever hears the door or the cough knows, by what is told, that the person is already on the way, and the house has time to get ready to receive them. That is why the belief is described as a kind of warning, closer to a sign of arrival than to a haunting. The article also notes that there are accounts from many parts of the country.

## In short

The vardøger is the sound, and more rarely the image, of someone who arrives before appearing. It is a spirit that warns, a relative of the fylgje, known in almost all of Norway and with equivalents in neighbouring countries.

## Terms

*Fylgje* is, in the north, the spirit that accompanies a person or a family. *Etiäinen* is the double of Finnish belief. An *entry* is an encyclopedia text on a subject.

## What remains uncertain

The text I read does not say who collected the oldest accounts of vardøger. The leksikon itself notes, in reply to a reader, that scientific language cannot confirm such experiences as facts, and so it describes them as belief. I also do not know, from these sources, whether the idea of a spirit that walks ahead already existed in the Viking Age.
