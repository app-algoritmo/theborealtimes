Galdra-Loftur means "Loftur the sorcerer". The man behind the nickname is Loftur Þorsteinsson, a student at the Latin school of Hólar, in the north of Iceland, and what is told of him is legend, with few facts from the archives.

## What is documented

According to the sources, Loftur was born around 1702, near Hvammsfjörður, in the northwest of Iceland. His father, Þorsteinn Jónsson, was a falconer at Vörðufell, and his mother was called Ásta Loftsdóttir. In 1716 he entered the school at Hólar, where he is said to have developed an interest in galdr, Norse magic. He left in 1722, and sources disagree on whether he graduated or was expelled. What came after is uncertain.

## The book Rauðskinna

The legend sets Loftur before a book, the Rauðskinna, "red skin", described as a grimoire. It is said to have been buried in 1520 with its author, Bishop Gottskálk grimmi Nikulásson, and that Loftur tried to obtain it and failed. None of this is a documented event.

## The end of the legend

In one version, the failure drove him mad. He was placed in the care of a priest at Staðarstaður, put out to sea in an old boat and vanished, presumably drowned. It is only one version among several, supported by a single Icelandic website, and the source gives no independent verification.

## The play

In 1914 the writer Jóhann Sigurjónsson made Loftur the hero of a play, "Galdra-Loftur", in Danish "Ønsket". It premiered on 26 December 1914 in Reykjavík and was published in Danish and Icelandic in 1915. There he appears as an ambitious scholar, in the manner of Faust, seeking power and knowledge through magic.
