Húsdrápa means "house poem". It is a skaldic poem attributed to Úlfr Uggason, an Icelandic poet of the later 10th century. It describes mythological scenes painted in a hall, and the stanzas that remain survive only as quotations in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

## The hall and the wedding

According to Laxdæla saga, Úlfr composed the poem for the wedding of Geirmundr and Þuríðr, daughter of Óláfr pái Höskuldsson. Óláfr had a hall built at Hjarðarholt, with legends painted on the walls and ceiling. The poem speaks of these paintings and of Óláfr himself. The saga calls it the "House Song" and says Óláfr rewarded the poet well.

## The three scenes

The surviving verses, twelve stanzas and half-stanzas, describe three myths: the funeral of Baldr, Thor fishing for Jörmungandr and the fight between Heimdallr and Loki over the necklace Brísingamen. This last scene is obscure, and Snorri understood it as a contest between the two over the necklace.

## The poet in other sagas

Úlfr also appears in Brennu-Njáls saga. In chapter 60 he loses a lawsuit and is challenged to a duel by Gunnar of Hlíðarendi. In chapter 102 he refuses Þorvaldr veili's request to kill the missionary Þangbrandr, and afterwards composes a lausavísa, a single loose stanza. The Landnámabók names his wife, Járngerðr.

## What remains

Of Úlfr's work, only the Húsdrápa and that single stanza survive. The poem is often compared with Haustlöng and Ragnarsdrápa, which also describe mythological works of art.

## In short

The Húsdrápa shows that around the year 1000 an Icelandic hall could be decorated with myths and a poet could be paid for describing them. To assemble the hall's panels, play [The painted hall](/game-the-painted-hall/).
