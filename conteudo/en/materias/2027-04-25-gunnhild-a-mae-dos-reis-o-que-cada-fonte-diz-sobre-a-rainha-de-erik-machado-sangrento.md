Gunnhild is called "Mother of Kings" because of the sons she had with Eric Bloodaxe. What is known of her comes from texts written long after the events, in large part by Icelanders, and each paints her differently. The honest way is to read each source separately.

## What Heimskringla says

In Heimskringla, by Snorri Sturluson, Gunnhild's father is Ozur Toti, a hersir of Hålogaland. She lives in a hut with two Finnish wizards and learns magic from them. The wizards kill other suitors, and Eric's men, returning from Bjarmaland, help her kill the wizards.

The work also says that Haakon the Good's rise followed the cruelty of Eric's rule and forced Eric and Gunnhild to flee to England. In one passage a kitchen boy spins round "through the sorcery of Gunnhild".

## What Egil's saga says

In Egil's saga, Eric wins Gunnhild, daughter of Ozur Toti, on an expedition to Bjarmaland. The poet Egill Skallagrímsson kills Bárðr of Atley around 930, and Gunnhild sends her brothers to try to kill Egill and his brother Thorolf. Egill kills both. To curse Eric and Gunnhild, he raises a horse's head on a pole, a níðstöng. Years later, in Jorvik, he composes the poem Höfuðlausn to save his life, and Eric forgives him, against her wishes.

## What the other sources say

Historia Norwegiae, from the 12th century, makes Gunnhild a daughter of Gorm the Old and Thyra. The article says modern scholars have largely accepted this version, which contradicts that of Heimskringla.

Laxdæla saga shows her as patron and lover of Hrut, an Icelandic chieftain at court, a generation younger than she. Jómsvíkinga saga says she returned to Denmark around 977 and was drowned in a bog on King Harald's orders.

That account led to a mix-up: the body of a woman found in a Jutland bog in 1835, the Haraldskær woman, was taken for her. Radiocarbon dating later showed she lived in the 6th century BCE.

## What scholars say

Gwyn Jones regarded many Icelandic traditions about Gunnhild as fictional, and the article notes that those sources were hostile to her. Marlene Ciklamini calls the wizards episode a "fable", and Jones sees it as a way of explaining her magic by tying her to the Finns. Even Gunnhild's historicity is debated.

## In short

Gunnhild is the mother of kings, wife of Eric Bloodaxe. Heimskringla tells of the Finnish wizards, Egil's saga makes her the poet's enemy, and Historia Norwegiae, Laxdæla saga and Jómsvíkinga saga bring different origin, love and end. The Haraldskær body is not hers.
