Líf and Lífþrasir are a couple of Norse mythology who, according to the texts, survive the end of the world hidden in a wood. This piece uses two entries of an encyclopaedia in English, one on the couple and one on the wood, and what they summarise of the medieval sources. When something is only one scholar's interpretation, that is said.

## What the texts say

In the Vafþrúðnismál, Odin asks the giant Vafþrúðnir who among men will survive the Fimbulvetr, the great winter. The giant answers that it will be Líf and Lífþrasir, hidden in Hoddmímis holt, who feed on the morning dew. The verses say that generations will spring from them. In the Gylfaginning, in chapter 53, the two hide during Surt's fire and afterwards repopulate the world.

## The names

*Líf* means "life" and is a female name. *Lífþrasir* is male, and a lexicon of 1860 explains it as "lover of life". The explanations for the second name are speculative, and the name of the couple is in itself surer than any reading of its meaning.

## Where the hiding place is

*Holt* is "wood", and Hoddmímis holt is Hoddmímir's wood. The scholar Carolyne Larrington suggests that Hoddmímir may be Mímir and that the couple may be hidden in Yggdrasil, and she presents this as a possibility. Rudolf Simek sees in the couple a repetition of the creation of humans, Askr and Embla, and identifies Hoddmímis holt with Yggdrasil. That is his interpretation. The entry also recalls that the idea of people born from a tree is old in Germanic regions, and cites a legend of a shepherd from Bavaria and the hero Örvar-Oddr, treated as a tree-man. The fate of Yggdrasil is not stated.

## In brief

There is a winter and there is a fire. In the midst of them, two people hide in a wood, eat dew and afterwards give rise to new generations. It is an end that contains a new beginning. The sources agree on this picture and differ on the exact place of the hiding and on the meaning of the names.

## Terms

*Fimbulvetr* is the great winter that comes before the end of the world. *Holt* is a wood. *Reduplication* is the repetition of one same theme. A *gloss* is the explanation of the sense of a word.

## What remains uncertain

It is not known who Hoddmímir is. The idea that he is Mímir is only a possibility. It is also uncertain whether the wood is the tree Yggdrasil itself, and the answer depends on the scholar. The etymologies of Lífþrasir are speculative. The texts summarised here do not say how the couple hide, and what happens to Yggdrasil during the fire is not declared. None of this proves or refutes a belief; it shows what the material allows one to affirm.
