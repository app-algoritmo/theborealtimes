Forseti is a god of Norse mythology tied to disputes and settlements. The New World Encyclopedia article that serves as the basis for this piece is short, and what it offers fits into a few points. It is worth separating what it says from what is often repeated elsewhere.

## The Norse source

The text rests Forseti's role on a passage of the *Gylfaginning*, the part of the *Prose Edda* in which Snorri Sturluson describes the gods. There, Forseti has a hall in heaven called Glitnir. Whoever brings a quarrel to him leaves reconciled: in the words quoted, "all these return thence reconciled". That sentence is what supports the image of the god as judge and mediator.

## The hall Glitnir

The name *Glitnir* means "shining". The article says the hall had a silver roof and golden pillars, and that its light could be seen from far away. This description comes from Orchard, a supporting work, and the article gives no other source for it.

## The name among the Frisians

In Frisia, the god appears as *Fosite* or *Foseti*, and the article says he is thought to have been the chief god or an ancestor of the Frisian people. It then offers a hypothesis: the cult would have reached Norway from Frisia around the year 700, crossing the Oslofjord. The text treats this as something "thought", not as established fact.

## The name

In Old Norse, *forseti* carries the sense of a "chairman" of a meeting, someone who presides over an assembly. In modern Icelandic and Faroese the word means "president", according to Lindow, cited in the article. That is why the name suggests someone who conducts a judgment.

## At the end of the world

The article notes that Forseti is not named as a combatant at Ragnarök. For that reason it is assumed that he would stay out of the battle, but this is a deduction, not something an ancient text says.

## Terms

*Gylfaginning* is the part of the *Prose Edda* that describes the gods; *Glitnir* is the name of the hall; *Fosite* or *Foseti* is the Frisian form of the name; *forseti*, in Old Norse, is one who presides. Together, these four terms sum up everything the article claims about the god: a hall, a sentence about reconciliation, a name that travels and a word that means to preside.

## What remains uncertain

The article gives no passage from the *Grímnismál* or the *Skáldskaparmál* for Forseti; the only Norse source it cites is the *Gylfaginning*. The Frisian origin and the journey of the cult are hypotheses. Forseti's absence from Ragnarök is a supposition. And the article itself leans on modern studies, not on the manuscripts, so a reader who wants to go further needs to open Snorri's texts and the studies it cites.
