In the early tenth century, the Rus, a people whom medieval sources associate with Scandinavia and who controlled routes between the Baltic and the Black Sea, signed a treaty with Byzantium. The text survived only in the *Primary Chronicle*, a medieval account of the history of the Rus. It is one of the oldest documents in which the names of eastern Norsemen can be read, and it helps us understand how the Rus negotiated with an empire.

## The text and the date

The treaty appears in the chronicle under the year 911. The historian Serhii Plokhy doubts the dates attached to treaties of this kind, but considers the text of 911 more reliable than those of 907 and 944, among other reasons because it has no list of Rus towns. Some scholars think there was a Greek original, translated into Old Church Slavonic and then lost.

## The envoys

The text gives the names of the Rus envoys, and most sound Scandinavian. The page consulted gives Norse forms such as Farulfr, Vermundr, Rollabʀ, Frilleifr, Ingjaldr, Karli, Karni, Leiðulfr, Hróaldr, Hróarr and Þróndr or Þrandr. The name Fastr, which appears in the chronicle as Fost, is given with an asterisk, which marks a reconstructed form. The list shows that at that time the Rus chiefs and merchants bore Norse names, even while living among Slavs.

## What the treaty regulates

The first article proclaims a "solid and durable" friendship and says that neither side will harm the other. Articles 3 to 7 deal with criminal law and with the colony of Rus merchants in Constantinople, including the inheritance of a merchant who dies in the city. Article 8 deals with maritime law. The following articles cover the ransom of prisoners, the exchange of criminals and the status of Varangian mercenaries in Byzantine service.

## Why it matters

The treaty shows that the Rus and Byzantium treated trade as a matter of law, with rules for crimes, inheritances and shipwrecks. And it shows that the group that negotiated was made up of men with Norse names, on behalf of a prince who ruled over Slavs.

## In short

The *Primary Chronicle* keeps a treaty of 911 between the Rus and Byzantium, with friendship, rules of law and the names of envoys largely Scandinavian. Some scholars question its authenticity, but the text of 911 is seen as more reliable than the others.

## Terms

*Rus* is the name that medieval sources give to the people, associated with Scandinavians, who controlled the first towns between the Baltic and Constantinople. *Varangians* are the Norsemen who served in Byzantium and in the Rus. *Primary Chronicle* is the medieval work in which the treaty is preserved.

## What remains uncertain

The authenticity and the date of the treaty are debated. It is not known whether the text we have is a faithful copy of an original, and the Norse forms of the names are reconstructions by scholars, not certain readings.
