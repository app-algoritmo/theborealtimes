The scene is famous, but worth rereading slowly: a wedding, a tree inside a hall, a stranger who walks in and out, and a sword nobody can pull free. It stands near the start of the Völsunga saga, written in Iceland in the 13th century, and sets up the whole story that follows.

## The hall and the tree

King Siggeir of Gothland asks King Volsung for the hand of his daughter Signy. She does not want it, but leaves the decision to her father, who agrees. Siggeir comes to Volsung's hall for the wedding feast with many important men. Great fires burn along the hall, and in the middle stands an enormous tree, the Barnstokkr, its trunk inside the room and its branches reaching through the roof.

## The one-eyed old man

One evening a stranger walks in. He has one eye, wears a spotted cloak and a slouched hat, and seems very old. He thrusts a sword into the trunk up to the hilt and says, in the translation of Morris and Magnússon: "Whoso draweth this sword from this stock shall have the same as a gift from me". He leaves without saying who he is. A translator's note identifies the old man as Odin, who gave an eye for a drink from Mimir's well.

## Who can do it

Everyone wants the sword, but nobody frees it, until Sigmund, Volsung's son, takes hold and draws it out with ease. Siggeir offers to buy it for three times its weight in gold. Sigmund refuses: the sword came to him, and it will never go to Siggeir. The king is offended, hides what he feels, and that very night begins planning revenge. Later in the saga the sword breaks, and its pieces will be remade as Gram, the sword of Sigurd.

## The name of the trunk

*Barnstokkr* means something like "child-trunk". Jesse Byock suggests the original name may have been *brandstokkr*, with *brandr*, "brand", a word sometimes used for "hearth", which would tie the tree to the fire in the hall. Byock also proposes that the Barnstokkr might be Yggdrasil, and Andy Orchard notes that a tree that supports and sprouts through the roof recalls Yggdrasil's position in relation to Valhalla. Richard Wagner reused the scene in the Ring: Siegmund pulls Nothung from the tree.

## In short

A one-eyed old man, who is Odin, thrusts a sword into the trunk in the hall. Only Sigmund draws it, and his refusal to sell it to Siggeir opens the revenge that drives the saga.

## Terms

*Barnstokkr* is the central trunk of the hall. *Goths* were Siggeir's people, here called Gothland in the English translation. *Gram* is the sword Sigurd will have later.

## What remains uncertain

The saga was written centuries after the events it tells, and the scene is legend, not history. The meaning of the name Barnstokkr is a hypothesis, and the identification with Yggdrasil is a scholars' proposal, not something the text says.
