Háttatal, "Tally of Metres", is the last of the sections of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda. It has about 20,000 words and is a treatise on skaldic metre.

## A manual made of examples

Most of the text is verse composed by Snorri himself, illustrating each form. He says how poetry should be made and, at the same time, describes what poets did, noting that "the older poets did not always" follow the rules.

## How the forms work

The forms depend on the number of syllables per line, and also on assonance, consonance and alliteration. End rhyme plays a minor role. The patterns can be described by letters, such as AAAAAAAA and AAAABBBB.

Háttatal is easier to follow alongside the First Grammatical Treatise. Scholars debate whether the text follows classical models or a logical, dialogue-based approach.

## What is known from elsewhere

According to what is known from other sources, the poem has 102 stanzas, in honour of King Hákon Hákonarson and Earl Skúli, around 1222 and 1223. The most common form, dróttkvætt, has eight lines of six syllables.

## In short

Háttatal shows the poet as a craftsman who could name every form he used. To stack the stanzas of the poem, play [Snorri's stanzas](/game-snorri-s-stanzas/).
