The Hiddensee treasure is a set of gold jewels found on an island of the Baltic Sea, in Germany. This piece uses two sources, an entry of an encyclopaedia in English and a report of the site The Local, which also speaks of a find of coins on the neighbouring island of Rügen. What is a hypothesis is said to be a hypothesis.

## The find

The jewels appeared in 1873, on the German island of Hiddensee, after floods that occurred in 1872 and 1873. The set has 16 pendants, a brooch and a neck ring, all of gold, and weighs about 600 grams. The dating is the 10th century.

## Hammer and cross

The pendants combine two motifs, Thor's hammer and the cross. The two symbols belong to different religions, the Old Norse and the Christian. The source describes the combination, and does not say what it meant to those who made or wore the jewels.

## Where it is today

The original is in the Stralsund Museum of Cultural History. A replica is in the Hiddensee Local History Museum. Anyone who wants to see the real pieces must therefore go to Stralsund.

## King Harald

The link of the treasure with King Harald Bluetooth is described as only "possible". In 2018, in Schaprode, on the island of Rügen, between 500 and 600 coins appeared, partly split, and about 100 of them are from Harald's reign. Michael Schirren said that a link is possible. This is not proof, and it does not prove that the gold of Hiddensee belonged to the king.

## How the sources complete each other

The entry describes the set, the weight, the dating and the museums. The report brings the find of coins on Rügen, from 2018, and the statement of Michael Schirren. Together, the two place the treasure beside coins of Harald's reign, which makes the link with the king possible to discuss, but not proven.

## In brief

A gold treasure of the 10th century, found after a flood, has pendants that mix Thor's hammer and the cross. The original is in Stralsund, and the possible link with Harald Bluetooth is supported by coins found on Rügen, but only as a possibility.

## Terms

A *pendant* is a piece hung on a cord. A *brooch* is a piece for fastening clothing. A *neck ring* is a ring worn at the neck. A *replica* is a copy.

## What remains uncertain

It is not known who made the set or to whom it belonged. The link with Harald Bluetooth is only a possibility, and the coins of Rügen do not confirm it. The mixture of hammer and cross is not explained in the sources. The news text carries the statement of Michael Schirren, who says only that a link is possible, and that is not a firm conclusion.
