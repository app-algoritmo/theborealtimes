Among the short tales of Icelandic literature, few speak so much about the craft of telling. The *Íslendings þáttr sögufróða*, "the tale of the story-wise Icelander", shows a young storyteller at the court of King Harald Sigurdsson, the Hardrada, and a saga he did not dare to tell. It is a tale about the worth of a good story and of the person who can tell it properly.

## A storyteller at court

King Haraldr Sigurðarson has a young Icelander in his service. His job is to entertain the king's guard, the *hirð*, by telling sagas. He does the work well and is rewarded. The page consulted does not give the young man's name.

## The sadness before Yule

When Yule, the *jól*, approaches, the Icelander grows sad. The king asks the reason. The young man says he has told every saga he knows except one, the one about Haraldr's own journeys abroad, and that he does not dare to tell it.

## The king's order

The king orders the Icelander to tell that saga at the Yule feast. The page consulted does not explain why the young man hesitates.

## The king's question

After hearing the saga, the king says he liked it and that it was faithful to the events. He then asks how the young man knows it. The Icelander answers that he learned it from Halldórr Snorrason, who travelled with Haraldr. The page consulted describes nothing beyond this exchange.

## The manuscripts

The tale survives in three manuscripts: *Morkinskinna*, *Hulda* and *Hrokkinskinna*. The page consulted does not associate it with the Flateyjarbók. One hypothesis is that it was written at the end of the thirteenth century and included in the *Morkinskinna* to give authority to the account of Haraldr's journeys abroad.

## Who has studied the tale

The page consulted cites Régis Boyer's French translation of 1999, in the book *Les Sagas miniatures (þættir)*, and a 2006 paper by Elena Gurevich on the fantastic element in the tales of Icelanders, the *Íslendinga þættir*. I did not see these works, and so I do not know what they say about this particular tale.

## The king of the tale

Harald Sigurdsson ruled Norway from 1046 to 1066 and became known as Hardrada, the "hard ruler". Before that, he served outside Norway, and it is those journeys that the tale refers to.

## In short

A young Icelander tells sagas at the court of Harald Hardrada and saves for Yule the one he fears most: the saga of the king's journeys. The king approves the faithfulness of the account and asks where it came from. The answer is Halldórr Snorrason.

## Terms

*Þáttr* is a short tale, usually inserted in a larger saga. *Hirð* is the personal guard of a king. *Jól* is Christmas, or rather the Norse winter feast.

## What remains uncertain

The page consulted does not give the Icelander's name, the detailed content of the saga he tells or what happens after the king's question. The date of composition, the end of the thirteenth century, is a hypothesis.
