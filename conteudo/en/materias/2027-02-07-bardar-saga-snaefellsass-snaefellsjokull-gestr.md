Bárðar saga Snæfellsáss, also called Bárðar saga Snæfellsáss ok Gests, is a late saga of Icelanders with legendary elements. The source consulted for this piece divides it into two parts, probably by different authors: chapters 1 to 10, the Bárðar saga, revolve around Bárðr, and chapters 11 to 20, the Gests saga Bárðarsonar, around his son, Gestr. The first part is set in Snæfellsnes, in Iceland. This piece separates what the source states from what it leaves open.

## Who Bárðr is

Bárðr's mother was human, and his father half *jötunn*, half troll. He was fostered by Dofri, the "mountain-dweller" of Dovrefjell. With his first wife, Flaumgerðr, Dofri's daughter, he had three tall, beautiful daughters: Helga, Þordís and Guðrún. With the second, Herþrúðr, he had six more daughters.

## The settlement and the quarrel

Bárðr emigrates to Iceland with his wife and daughters, arrives at a lagoon on the south coast of Snæfellsnes, which he names Djúpalón, and builds the farm Laugarbrekka. His half-brother, Þorkell, lives at Arnarstapi and has two sons, Rauðfeldr and Sölvi. During a period of drifting ice, Rauðfeldr pushes Helga out to sea on a block of ice. She ends up in Greenland, where she finds a lover. Enraged, Bárðr pushes Rauðfeldr into the Rauðfeldsgjá ravine and throws Sölvi off the cliff Sölvahamar. Bárðr and Þorkell fight, and Þorkell's leg is broken. He leaves the district.

## The guardian

Bárðr gives away his lands and vanishes into the ice cap of Snæfellsjökull. From then on he is known as Bárðr Snæfellsáss, a "guardian spirit" of Snæfell. The source says the people of the peninsula practically worshipped him, called on him in hardship and found him a real help. He wanders the region in a grey cowl, with a walrus-hide rope and a cleft staff with a long gaff for use on glaciers. He brings Helga back from Greenland, but she pines for her lover and cannot stay with her father. Called on by a man of Ingjaldshvoll, he rows out alone to rescue him from a dangerous fishing spot. Bárðr and Þorkell, according to the saga, make peace.

## Gestr and Raknarr's mound

In the second part, Gestr, "guest", has a dog named Snati. He travels to the court of Olaf Tryggvason in Norway, where the king asks him, at Christmas, to accept the challenge of the draugr of King Raknarr of Helluland. The expedition opens the mound on a deserted island in the north. Gestr calls on his father, who appears but cannot help, and then on King Olaf, whose light drains Raknarr's strength. Back at court, Gestr is baptised. That night, his father appears in a dream and destroys both his eyes, and Gestr dies the next day.

## Terms

*Jötunn* is a giant. A *draugr* is an undead creature. *Snæfellsáss* means "ás of Snæfell". A *late saga* is a saga written later, with legendary elements.

## What remains uncertain

The probable date is the early 14th century, but Halldór Hermansson proposed around 900, an outlier. The paternity of Sólrún, who claims to be Gestr's daughter, is disputed within the saga itself, which states there is no record of children of his. As for Grímr, the mysterious fisherman, all that is recorded is that people suspected he was Thor. The worship of Bárðr is described as popular belief, not as verified practice.
