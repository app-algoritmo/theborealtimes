*Draumkvedet*, the "Dream Poem", is a Norwegian ballad of oral tradition, from the late Middle Ages, preserved in many versions from Telemark. The protagonist is Olav Åsteson, who sleeps for a long time and, on waking, tells what he saw in the other world. This piece uses two sources, an encyclopaedia in English and the Store norske leksikon, and separates what they state from what they leave open.

## What the ballad is

The first source identifies it as a late medieval ballad, catalogued in the indexes of Norwegian ballads as NMB 54 and TSB B 31. The records that have reached us were made in the second half of the 19th century, in Lårdal and Kviteseid, in Telemark. The second source places the first collections in the 1840s, with Jørgen Moe and Landstad, and says the poem has a refrain, the *omkved*.

## The plot

According to the first source, Olav Åsteson sleeps from Christmas Eve to the thirteenth day. During the sleep, he sees a moor of thorns, a very tall bridge, a black fire, hell and part of heaven. The ballad also carries advice on charity. The second source adds that Christ and Saint Michael appear, and a figure called Grutte Gråskjeggje.

## How it was preserved

The second source says there are more than a hundred recordings, mostly fragments. Moltke Moe reconstructed a version, and one of the best-known versions is that of Maren Ramskeid. The source notes that the poem gained a new artistic life: Agnes Buen Garnås received the Spellemannsprisen in 1984 for a recording, and there have been performances at the Fantoft stave church since the mid-1990s. The first source mentions compositions made from the ballad.

## How it has been read

The second source reports that the poem probably drew on Irish and Scottish visionary accounts. It also cites the opinion of Olav Bø, for whom *Draumkvedet* is "Norway's answer to Dante". It is a comparison of reading, not a claim that the works are directly related.

## Terms

A *ballad* is a sung poem that tells a story. The *omkved* is the refrain that repeats in the Norwegian ballad. A *vision* is an account of an experience of the beyond. *Oral tradition* is what passes from person to person by speech and song.

## What remains uncertain

There is no medieval manuscript, and the age of the ballad is estimated by those who study it, not documented. The version known today is a gathering of fragments, and Moltke Moe's reconstruction shows that there are editorial choices among the voices. The Irish and Scottish influence is described as probable, and the comparison with Dante is an opinion. The sources do not say why the poem was preserved precisely in Telemark.
