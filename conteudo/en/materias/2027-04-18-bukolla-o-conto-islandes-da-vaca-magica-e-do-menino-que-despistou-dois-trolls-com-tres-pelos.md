Búkolla is one of the best-known folktales in Iceland, and there are many children's books about her in the country. The heroine is a cow. The hero is a boy who does not even have a name. And the villains are two trolls, a big one and a small one.

## The cow vanishes

An old couple lives with their son, of whom they are not very fond, and depends on the cow Búkolla. One day she disappears, while the old woman steps away. The parents send the boy to find her, and forbid him to return without the cow.

The boy goes out calling her name. Búkolla's bellows grow louder, until he finds her chained in a cave, under some rocks, and frees her.

## The escape

The trolls notice and go after them. Búkolla speaks only to give the boy instructions, one for each transformation. The first is to pull a hair from her tail and drop it behind them.

- **The first hair** becomes a river so wide that only a bird could cross it. The big troll sends the small one to fetch "my father's giant bull", and the bull drinks the whole river.
- **The second hair** becomes a wall of fire. The bull spits the water it drank over the flames and puts them out.
- **The third hair** becomes a tall mountain. The small troll brings a bore-iron, and the big troll drills through the mountain. He tries to squeeze through the narrow hole, gets stuck and turns to stone.

The boy goes home with Búkolla. The parents are glad to see them both. The troll stays inside the mountain for ever.

## What the tale does

The magic flight is a well-known pattern in folktales: the hero throws objects behind him, and each one turns into an obstacle. Here they are three hairs, which suits the tale's rule of three. The Icelandic touch is in the setting (rocks, caves, cracks) and in the help of an animal, who is smarter than the adults. What makes it fun is that the giant is beaten by his own haste: he wants to go through the mountain instead of around it.

Another point is the reversal. The parents send the boy away without the cow, and the boy nobody values is the one who saves her. In the end, he is the one who brings their livelihood back.

## In short

Búkolla is the tale of the cow stolen by trolls and the boy who frees her. Three hairs from her tail become a river, fire and a mountain, and the big troll ends up stuck in the rock. The source used here is a summary of the tale, and there are many Icelandic versions and children's books that retell it.
