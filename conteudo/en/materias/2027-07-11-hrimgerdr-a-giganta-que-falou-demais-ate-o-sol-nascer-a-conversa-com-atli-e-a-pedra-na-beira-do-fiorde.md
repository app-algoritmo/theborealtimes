Among the heroic poems of the Poetic Edda is the Helgakviða Hjörvarðssonar, "the poem of Helgi, son of Hjörvarðr". It tells the story of Prince Helgi, who fights to win the valkyrie Svava. Inside it is a famous scene in which a sailor faces a giantess with words alone.

## The night in the fjord

Helgi and his ships are anchored in a fjord, during the night. A giantess, Hrímgerðr, appears on the shore. The name combines hrím, "frost", with a second part that in other texts appears in the names of women and goddesses. She is a danger to the ships, and her arrival puts the men on alert.

## The conversation with Atli

The one who goes out to talk to her is Atli, one of Helgi's men. The two trade insults in verse. This is what scholars call flyting, a duel of insults that Norse poems liked to tell. Atli asks who she is and what she wants, and she answers with threats and scorn. He answers in kind, and the dialogue stretches through the early morning.

## The dawn

Atli's trick is simple: he keeps talking. According to the poem, when the sun rises, its rays reach the giantess, and she turns to stone. The image is part of a common idea in Norse stories: creatures of the night, such as giants and dwarfs, cannot bear daylight. Other texts of the Edda bring the same fate to dwarfs caught by the dawn.

So the lesson of Hrímgerðr is not strength, it is time: whoever wants to beat a giant can gain hours with a good conversation.
