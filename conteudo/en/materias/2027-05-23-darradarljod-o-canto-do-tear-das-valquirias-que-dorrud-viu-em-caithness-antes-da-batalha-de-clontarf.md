Darraðarljóð is a skaldic poem of 11 stanzas, preserved in chapter 157 of Njáls saga. It describes a vision had by a man named Dörruð, in which twelve valkyries weave at a loom and decide who will die in the battle of Clontarf, fought near Dublin in 1014.

## Dörruð's vision

In the saga the scene is set in Caithness, in the north of Scotland. Dörruð has a vision: twelve women stand around a loom and sing as they work. What they sing is the poem. It speaks of a battle under way and of who will fall in it, and the song goes along with the work of weaving.

## The loom

The loom of the poem is made from parts of warriors: entrails serve as warp and weft, swords as treadles, an arrow as the batten, and heads as the weights. It is the image of the warp-weighted loom, common among the Scandinavians of the north: the threads hang from a beam and are held taut by weights. Here, the cloth being woven is the fate of the battle.

## The valkyries

The English the source article names six of the twelve women: Hildr, Hjörþrimul, Sanngriðr, Svipul, Guðr and Göndul. These are names of valkyries, the choosers of the slain.

## Celtic and Norse

Crawford (1987) describes the story as a "powerful mixture of Celtic and Old Norse imagery". The scene lies in a region where the Norse and Gaelic worlds met, and the battle takes place on Irish soil.

## After the poem

Simek (2007) suggests that the poem may have influenced the three witches of Macbeth. In 1768 Thomas Gray paraphrased it in "The Fatal Sisters: An Ode". The Icelandic composer Jón Leifs set it to music in 1964, in a work for mixed chorus and orchestra (op. 60) that only premiered on 6 March 2025 in the Harpa concert hall in Reykjavík. Einar Selvik and Trevor Morris used stanzas from it in the opening battle of the second season of the series Vikings.
