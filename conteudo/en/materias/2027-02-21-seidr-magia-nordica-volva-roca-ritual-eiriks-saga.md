Seiðr was a magic practice of the Norse world, and whoever practised it, in the picture given by the sources, entered a trance with a ritual object in hand. This piece uses two sources, an entry of the site norse-mythology.org and an entry of HandWiki on the völur, the seeresses. The old sources on the subject are few, and the doubts are marked.

## The word and the practice

*Seiðr* means "cord, string, snare". According to the first entry, whoever practised it held a ritual distaff and entered a trance. Seiðr served for prophecy, blessing, cursing, healing and control of the weather, and the source cites Neil Price, from 2002, on this. HandWiki presents the origin of the name, the thread spun with a distaff, only as a theory.

## Freyja, Odin and the charge of ergi

Freyja is the model of the seeress, the völva, and Odin is the best-known male practitioner. Men who practised it could be called *argr*, that is, "unmanly", and the word comes with the idea of *ergi*. In the Lokasenna, stanza 24, Odin is accused of this.

## The völva Þorbjörg

The most detailed account is in Eiríks saga rauða, chapter 4. Around the year 1000, during a famine in Greenland, the völva Þorbjörg is summoned, and the household prepares to receive her. According to HandWiki, she carries a staff adorned with brass and gems and wears a blue or black cloak. She sits on a raised platform while young women sing to summon the powers. The saga says that she foresees the future and that the famine ends.

## The staff and the graves

The word *völva* is read as "wand carrier". The staff was the *seiðstafr*. HandWiki tells that staffs have been found in about 40 graves of women, usually rich ones. Many have the top in the shape of a basket, like that of a distaff, and some cannot be linked to a distaff and may be phallic. Among the finds cited are Fyrkat, in Denmark, Oseberg, in Norway, Köpingsvik, on Öland, and Hagebyhöga, in Sweden.

## In brief

Seiðr joins a thread, a staff and a trance. The texts show the practice through the eyes of those who wrote centuries later, and the graves show objects, not beliefs. When the two meet, as in the staff and the saga, the picture grows stronger, but not complete.

## Terms

*Seiðr* is the Norse magic practice. *Völva* is the seeress. A *distaff* is the rod on which wool is held for spinning. A *trance* is an altered state of consciousness. *Argr* is a word of insult tied to "unmanly".

## What remains uncertain

The sources are sparse, and almost everything comes from texts written after the Viking Age. The origin of the name, the link to the distaff, is only a theory. HandWiki says that Harald Bluetooth "apparently" kept a völva at Fyrkat, and the text also marks parts as lacking a citation. It is not known how the ritual really took place, and the graves do not show what people did with the staffs.
