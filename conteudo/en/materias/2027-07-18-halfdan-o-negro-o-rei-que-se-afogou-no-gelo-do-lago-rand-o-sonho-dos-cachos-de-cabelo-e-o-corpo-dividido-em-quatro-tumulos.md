Among the kings at the start of the *Heimskringla*, Halfdan the Black has one of the most quoted deaths. According to Snorri Sturluson, who wrote around 1230, he drowned in spring, at forty, when the ice of a lake gave way. He is the father of Harald Fairhair, the king whom tradition presents as the unifier of Norway.

## The young king

Snorri tells that Halfdan was one year old when his father, Gudrod, was killed. His mother, Åsa, took him to Agder, the kingdom of her father, Harald. At eighteen, Halfdan took over Agder and then went to Vestfold, which he divided with his brother Olaf. In the saga he extends his rule through Vingulmark, where he defeats the sons of King Gandalf, Hysing and Helsing, and through Raumarike and Hedemark, where he fights the kings Sigtryg and Eystein. He also claims Sogn, which had belonged to his father-in-law.

The saga gives him two marriages. The first, to Ragnhild, daughter of Harald Goldbeard, king of Sogn, produced a son who died at ten. The second, to Ragnhild, daughter of King Sigurd Hjort of Ringerike, produced Harald, the future Fairhair. Snorri also describes him as a lawgiver, who set penalties according to each person's rank.

## The two dreams

One of the best-known passages is that of the dreams. Ragnhild dreamed that a thorn taken from her dress grew into a great tree, with roots red with blood, a green trunk and branches white as snow, covering all of Norway. Halfdan, who rarely dreamed, slept in a pigsty on the advice of Thorleif the Wise, and dreamed of beautiful locks of hair, one of them more beautiful than the others. Thorleif read the dream as a sign of numerous descendants, with one descendant more famous than the rest.

## The ice gave way

The death comes after a feast in Hadeland. It was spring, the time of the thaw. The king's sleigh crossed the lake called Rand, through the bight of Rykinsvik, where the ice was weak. Snorri says the ice broke and that Halfdan and many people drowned.

The body was taken to Ringerike. Chiefs from Raumarike, Vestfold and Hedemark came to claim it, because it was believed that whoever kept the body would have good harvests. It was divided into four parts. The head was buried in a mound at Stein, in Ringerike, and the other three parts in mounds in each region, which came to be called Halfdan's Mounds.

## What is legend

Snorri wrote centuries after the events, and many scholars treat much of this narrative as legendary tradition. What it shows clearly is the belief that a king's body brought luck to the land where it rested.
