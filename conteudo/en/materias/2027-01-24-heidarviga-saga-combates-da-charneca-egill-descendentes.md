The *Heiðarvíga saga*, "the story of the heath-slayings", is one of the sagas of Icelanders. The source consulted for this piece describes it as badly preserved, and that is the first thing the reader needs to know: part of the text was lost and is known only through a summary. This piece keeps to what the article carries, which is little, and says so where necessary.

## A badly preserved saga

According to the article, the saga is "badly preserved". Twelve leaves of its only surviving manuscript were destroyed, together with their only copy, in the fire of Copenhagen in 1728. For that reason it reaches the reader with a hole in the middle.

## The summary from memory

The lost part is known only through a summary written from memory by the Icelandic scholar Jón Grunnvíkingur (1705–1779). That summary is the only surviving form of that content. The article says no more about how it was made, and this piece does not deduce what the summary may have left out.

## What it tells

The saga follows the descendants of Egill Skallagrímsson. Long-standing disputes and conflicts between them culminate in a battle and subsequent slayings on the heath, which give the saga its name. The article names no other characters and gives no further detail of the plot.

## One of the oldest?

Some scholars consider that the saga may be among the oldest sagas of Icelanders. The article gives no firm date and does not explain the reasoning behind this idea, so it stays as the opinion of part of the scholars.

## Modern readings

The article lists recent studies. Joanne Shortt Butler (2020) examines how the gaps in the text affect the reader's engagement with the narrative. Alexander Wilson (2022) discusses the "dissonant voices" in the saga's prosimetrum, that is, in its mix of prose and verse. Jesse Byock (1993) treats feud in the Icelandic sagas, which bears on the disputes of the story. Viðar Hreinsson (1997) included the saga in a translation of complete sagas, and the Icelandic Saga Database offers the text and an English translation.

## Terms

*Heiðarvíga* means "of the heath-slayings". *Prosimetrum* is the mix of prose and verse in a single work. A *lacuna* is a stretch missing from a text. Jón Grunnvíkingur is the 18th-century scholar whose summary preserves the burned part.
