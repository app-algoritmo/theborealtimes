The primstav is a wooden calendar used in Norway from the 14th to the 19th century. It is also known as a runestav, "rune staff", but instead of runes it carries images, which mark the religious feasts of fixed date.

## How it works

Most have the shape of a ruler or a sword, and some are round, oval or tablet-shaped. Others are made of thin slats joined like a book. Each day is marked by a carved notch, and every seventh day gets an extra mark. The stick is turned to the summer side on 14 April and to the winter side on 14 October, and nearly all have both sides.

## The symbols

A stick shows about 55 feast symbols, nearly all in honour of a saint, usually on the day of his or her death, and the symbol is often the saint's attribute. There is St Olav's axe, St Hallvard's millstone, St Lawrence's gridiron and St Peter's key. Crosses are common, and a tree is the most frequent symbol for the feasts of Mary.

## The oldest examples

The oldest dated example is from 1457 and is the only one from the 15th century. A few are from the 16th. Dated examples become more numerous in the 17th century and decline after 1700.

## The end of a calendar

Primstaver followed the Julian calendar. When the Gregorian calendar was introduced in Norway, in 1700, the feasts began to fall several days earlier, and the stick became hard to use. Some were still in use in the 19th century, mainly as a reminder of farm and fishing tasks.
