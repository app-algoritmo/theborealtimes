Hildisvíni means "battle swine", and it is the name of the boar that the goddess Freyja rides. The story that tells most about it comes from the poem Hyndluljóð, preserved in the Flateyjarbók, an Icelandic manuscript of the 14th century, and often included among the poems of the Poetic Edda.

## The disguise

In the poem, Freyja wants to help her protégé Óttar learn his own ancestry. To do so, she goes to visit the seeress Hyndla. She rides the boar Hildisvíni, who, according to the poem, is actually Óttar in the shape of a pig. Freyja says dwarves made the animal, a detail the poem gives with their names.

## The interrogation

Hyndla does not want to help. Freyja presses her and forces her to recite Óttar's lineage, generation by generation, which takes up most of the poem. The scene is what gives the poem its interest: a goddess who travels and argues to secure the memory of a mortal.

## The helmet of the same name

Hildisvín also appears in the Skáldskaparmál by Snorri Sturluson. He lists the name among the boar-names used for helmets, and says Hildisvín was the helmet of Áli. The Swedish king Aðils took it after winning the battle on the ice of Lake Vänern.

## Not to be confused with Gullinbursti

The golden boar of the god Freyr, Gullinbursti, is another animal, with another story. Hildisvíni is Freyja's animal, and it is the one that appears in the poem of Hyndla.

## In short

Hildisvíni is a brief figure that says much: Óttar's disguise, Freyja's journey and the same name on a war helmet. To help Óttar gather his ancestors, play [Óttar's ancestors](/game-ottar-s-ancestors/).
