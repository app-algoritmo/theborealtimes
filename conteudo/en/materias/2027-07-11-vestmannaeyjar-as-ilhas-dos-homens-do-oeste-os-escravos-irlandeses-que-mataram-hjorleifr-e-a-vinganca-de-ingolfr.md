Off the south coast of Iceland lie the Vestmannaeyjar, a group of about fifteen islands and skerries of volcanic origin. The name means "islands of the men of the west". The Norse called the Irish and the British, who lived west of the Norwegian Sea, vestmenn, "men of the west". According to the Landnámabók, the reason for the name lies in a revenge.

## Hjörleifr and the slaves

Hjörleifr Hróðmarsson was the brother-in-law of Ingólfr Arnarson, the first settler of Iceland. Hjörleifr went to Iceland and settled on the south coast, on a headland that still bears his name, Hjörleifshöfði. Earlier, after an expedition to Ireland, he had brought Irish slaves with him.

In the spring, according to the saga, Hjörleifr wanted to sow and made the slaves pull the plough, because he had only one ox. The slaves killed the ox and said that a bear had killed it. When Hjörleifr and his men went out to look for the bear, the slaves attacked them one by one and killed them. Afterwards they took the women and the goods and fled by boat to some islands to the south.

## Ingólfr's revenge

Ingólfr found his brother-in-law dead and set off after the fugitives. He found them on the islands, where they were holding a feast, and killed them. One of them, called Dufþakr, leapt from a cliff, and the place became known as Dufþaksskor. Since the killers were Irish, the islands came to be known as the islands of the men of the west.

## What we know

The account comes from the Landnámabók, written centuries after the settlement. Historians therefore read it with caution, seeing it more as tradition than as an exact record. Even so, the name of the islands is a fact, and it confirms the presence of Irish people among the country's first inhabitants.
