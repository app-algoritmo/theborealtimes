The papar are "Christian men" mentioned in the medieval sources of Iceland as inhabitants of the island before the arrival of the Norse. This piece uses two sources, an entry of an encyclopaedia in English and a text from the site Hurstwic on the settlement of Iceland, and separates what they state from what they leave open.

## What the written sources say

The *Íslendingabók*, by Ari, written between 1122 and 1133, states that there were on the island "Christian men", called papar, who left when the Norse arrived. The *Landnámabók* tells that Irish monks left books, bells and crosiers. The first source also cites Dicuil, who speaks of "holy men", and the *Historia Norwegiæ*, which describes the papar differently. It also mentions Grímr Kamban and the *Færeyinga saga*.

## Place names

There are place names that carry the word papar: Papey and Papafjörður, in Iceland, and Paparøkur, in the Faroe Islands, among others. The source cites them as a sign that the name circulated, and not as proof of who lived there.

## The dating of the settlement

The second source places the beginning of the Norse settlement in 874, with Ingólfur, according to the *Landnámabók*. Tephrochronology, which dates layers of volcanic ash, supports a date around 872. The settlement would have been complete by about 930.

## What archaeology has shown

The second source states that archaeology has not proved the presence of the papar. Ruins called Írskra búðir, attributed to them in tradition, are later than the Norse settlement. The source also cites traces in names such as Papafjörður.

## Two sources, two roles

The first source gathers the medieval texts and the place names, and shows that the idea of the papar appears in more than one text and in more than one place in the North Atlantic. The second source looks at the settlement: it gives the dates, 874 and about 872 by the ash, and says what archaeology has found and not found. Together they show the written tradition on one side and the lack of material confirmation on the other.

## Terms

*Papar* is the word used in the sources for the "Christian men" of the island. A *crosier* is a cleric's staff. *Tephrochronology* is dating by layers of volcanic ash. A *place name* is the name of a locality.

## What remains uncertain

Neither source demonstrates that the papar lived in Iceland. The written ones are late in relation to the settlement, since the *Íslendingabók* is from the 12th century, and archaeology has not confirmed their presence. The sources differ in how they describe them: Irish monks, in the *Landnámabók*, and another description in the *Historia Norwegiæ*. The place names show that the word existed, but not that anyone lived in those places. It is also not known how long they would have stayed or where they went.
