Ragnarsdrápa means "drápa about Ragnarr". It is a skaldic poem attributed to Bragi inn gamli Boddason, the "oldest known skald", who lived in the 9th century. It describes mythological scenes on a decorated shield that a man named Ragnar gave to the poet.

## Who Ragnar was

The refrain says that Ragnar gave Bragi the shield and "a multitude of stories". Stanza 2 calls Ragnar the son of Sigurðr. Snorri Sturluson identified him with the Danish king Ragnarr Lodbrok, whose father was said to be Sigurd Ring, and called the poem Ragnars drápa loðbrókar. Vésteinn Ólason argues that the poem was addressed to a later Ragnar. Earlier scholars even thought it was composed for the Swedish king Björn at Haugi, at whose court Bragi would have been a skald.

## The scenes on the shield

Two scenes are explicitly attributed to the poem. In the first, the brothers Hamðir and Sörli attack king Jörmunrekkr, the Ermanaric of the Gothic sources. In the second, Heðinn and Högni fight the never-ending battle, the Hjaðningavíg. Scholars infer that the poem also included Thor's fishing for Jörmungandr and Gefjun's ploughing of Zealand out of Swedish soil, but this is not stated explicitly.

## What survived

The fragments that remain come from Snorri's Prose Edda. In a complete poem there would have been four stanzas for each of the four quarters of the shield.

## Form

The poem uses the dróttkvætt metre, well executed but with rhyme rules that would only be fixed later: in the odd lines hending is often missing, and in the even lines there is often only skothending, while later poetry would require aðalhending in the even lines. The language has archaic and complex kennings that strain the syntax. This suggests that the skalds' art already had a long history before Bragi.

## In short

Ragnarsdrápa is one of the first windows onto skaldic poetry, and its shield, with its scenes, links it to Haustlöng and Húsdrápa. To see what the poem says about each thing, play [Bragi's shield](/game-bragi-s-shield/).
