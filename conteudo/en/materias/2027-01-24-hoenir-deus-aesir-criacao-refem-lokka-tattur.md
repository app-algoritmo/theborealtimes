Hœnir is one of the Æsir, the principal gods of Norse mythology, and a figure with few appearances. The English Wikipedia article this piece relies on gathers what the sources say about him: a passage on creation, a stay as hostage, a protective role in a ballad from the Faroe Islands and a hypothesis that ties him to birds. It helps to read each point separately, because the texts do not agree with one another.

## One of three who made humans

In the *Völuspá*, Hœnir is one of three gods who, with Odin and Lóðurr, create the first human beings, Ask and Embla. The article describes him as assisting Odin and Lóðurr in that act. The same text says he is one of the few gods who survive Ragnarök.

## What Snorri tells

The *Prose Edda* says that Hœnir gave reason to humankind; the article cites MacCulloch (2005) for this. In the *Gylfaginning*, however, the account of creation names Vili and Vé in place of Hœnir. Because Snorri Sturluson knew the *Völuspá*, the article notes that Hœnir may be another name for Vili, and it presents this only as a possibility.

## The hostage among the Vanir

According to the *Ynglinga saga*, after the war between the Æsir and the Vanir, Hœnir and Mímir were sent to the Vanir as hostages to seal a truce. On arrival in Vanaheim, Hœnir was described as large and handsome, and the Vanir judged him well suited to be a chief, so they made him one at once. But he was indecisive and depended on Mímir for every decision. When Mímir was absent, Hœnir answered with noncommittal grunts.

## Other appearances

Hœnir has minor roles in the *Haustlöng* and the *Reginsmál*. In a medieval Faroese ballad, *Lokka táttur*, he protects a farmer's boy by summoning seven swans. For that ballad the article cites Joseph S. Hopkins (2021).

## The name and the birds

Scholars link the name Hœnir to the epithets *langifótr*, "long-legs", and *aurkonungr*, "mud-king", and to words in other languages: the Greek κύκνος, "swan", and the Sanskrit *śakuna*, "bird of omen". Viktor Rydberg and others, including Gudbrand Vigfusson, read these clues as pointing to the stork. As support the article lists the European folk legend that storks deliver babies and Hœnir's role in the Faroese ballad, where he calls on swans.

## Terms

The *Æsir* are the main group of gods, and the *Vanir* are the other group, with whom the Æsir went to war. The *Völuspá* is the poem that names Hœnir among the makers of humans, and the *Ynglinga saga* is the one that tells the story of the hostage. *Lokka táttur* is the Faroese ballad of the seven swans. *Langifótr* is an epithet meaning "long-legs". These few terms sum up everything the article claims: a helper at the creation, a chief who does not decide alone, a protection carried out by birds and a name that may come from them.

## What remains uncertain

The idea that Hœnir and Vili are the same god is a possibility raised from Snorri's knowledge of the *Völuspá*, and nothing more. The link between the name and the stork is a scholars' hypothesis, argued by Rydberg in 1886, and the article presents it as a hypothesis. There are also no manuscripts or annotated editions of the poems here: this piece rests on a single encyclopedic article, and anyone who wants to go further has to open the *Völuspá*, the *Ynglinga saga* and the Faroese ballad.
