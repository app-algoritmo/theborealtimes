The *Vatnsdæla saga* is one of the sagas of Icelanders, and the source consulted for this piece describes it, in essence, as a family chronicle about the residents of Vatnsdalur. The valley runs south from Húnaflói, a large bay in northern Iceland. The article is short, and this piece keeps to what it carries, without adding episodes that it does not mention.

## A family chronicle

The saga follows several generations of a family that came from Norway and settled in northern Iceland. The narrative runs until the arrival of Christianity in the late 10th century. For that reason it is, above all, the story of a place and of those who lived in it, more than the account of a single life.

## Ingimundr and the king

The principal protagonist is Ingimundr Þorsteinsson. He fought for Harald Fairhair at the battle of Hafrsfjord, won the king's friendship and received an amulet. The article cites the amulet as part of his relationship with the king, and nothing more is said about it here.

## The sorceress and the move

At the instigation of a sorceress, Ingimundr moved to Iceland and settled at Vatnsdalur, in Húnaþing. The article gives no details of the conversation or of what the sorceress said, and this piece does not invent them.

## The manuscripts

A fragment is preserved in a late 14th- or early 15th-century manuscript, AM 445 b 4to. Complete versions exist only in later paper manuscripts: AM 559 4to, written between 1686 and 1688, and AM 942 4to, written between 1700 and 1782.

## The date

The complete texts likely derive from a 14th-century source. The saga may have been originally written in the late 13th century. The article cites Pulsiano (1993), *Medieval Scandinavia: an encyclopedia*, for this discussion, and it does not choose between the two dates.

## Further reading

The article lists Jane Smiley's 2001 translation, in Penguin Classics, within *The Sagas of the Icelanders*. It also lists a study by Richard L. Harris (2010) on the saga's proverbs and the Sword of Jökull, which relates them to the flawed heroism of Grettir Ásmundarson. The full text is in the Icelandic Saga Database.

## Terms

A *saga of Icelanders* is the genre of prose narratives about Icelandic families. *Vatnsdalur* is the valley, and *Húnaflói* is the bay from which it stretches. *Húnaþing* is the district. *AM* marks manuscripts of the Árni Magnússon collection. These terms are enough to place the reader, and what lies beyond them depends on opening the saga.

## What remains uncertain

It is not known which of the dates, late 13th century or 14th century, is right for the original composition, and the article does not settle the question.
