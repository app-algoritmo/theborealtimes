When you visit Kirkwall, the red sandstone cathedral dominates the town. Its name is St Magnus, but the name usually tied to the building is another: Rögnvald Kali Kolsson, earl of Orkney in the 12th century.

## Who Rögnvald was

Rögnvald Kali Kolsson (c. 1100–1158) was a Norwegian earl of Orkney and Shetland who came to be venerated as a saint. Two Orkney islands, North Ronaldsay and South Ronaldsay, bear his name. His father was the *lendmann* Kolr Kalisson, and his mother Gunnhildr Erlendsdóttir. The page consulted says he may have been born in Jæren but considers this unlikely, and suggests the area of Fjære church or the royal estate at Lista, both in Agder.

## The claim to Orkney

The claim came through his mother, Gunnhildr, sister of Magnus Erlendsson, earl of Orkney. In 1129 King Sigurd I appointed him earl of Orkney and Shetland. He was to receive half of Orkney, as Magnus had, but his second cousin Páll Hákonarson ruled alone and refused to share. In 1136 Rögnvald was hailed as earl while serving King Harald Gille. In 1138 he appointed his ward Harald Maddadsson, Páll's nephew, as co-earl.

## The cathedral

According to the page consulted, Rögnvald began the construction of St Magnus Cathedral in Kirkwall in 1137. The start of the work, in 1137, falls between the acclamation of 1136 and the appointment of the co-earl in 1138. The page mentions no vow in honour of his uncle, nor does it say how the work was financed. Claims on this do not appear in that source and are therefore left out of this text.

## The journey to the Holy Land

In 1151 he went to the Holy Land. The *Orkneyinga saga* devotes five chapters to the journey, but says little about the stay. It attributes the impulse to Eindriði Ungi, a distant relative, and describes prestige as one of the motives. While he was away, David I of Scotland granted half of Caithness to Erlendr Haraldsson, who would be killed in 1156.

## Death and memory

In August 1158, Rögnvald and eight men were killed by Thorbjörn Klerk, a former friend of Harald whom Rögnvald had outlawed for a murder in Kirkwall. He was buried in St Magnus Cathedral. Miracles are reported at the grave and at the stone where he died, but the page does not verify them. He was also, with Hallr Þórarinsson, author of the skaldic poem *Háttalykill inn forni*.

## What can be said with care

Uncertain remain the exact place of birth, the financing of the cathedral, any vow and the canonisation: the page says Pope Celestine III canonised him in 1192, but notes that apparently no surviving record confirms it.
