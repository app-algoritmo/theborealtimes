Hjalti Skeggjason was an Icelandic chieftain who lived through the turn of the year 1000. According to the sagas, at the Althing of 999 he recited verses that mocked the goddess Freyja, and the assembled chieftains sentenced him for blasphemy. The penalty was lesser outlawry: he would have to leave Iceland for a time. In Njáls saga the verse calls Freyja a bitch and Odin a dog, or the other way round.

## The journey to Norway

Hjalti set out with his father-in-law, Gizurr the White, to serve the sentence in Norway. There King Ólafr Tryggvason was holding Icelanders prisoner after the missionary Þangbrandr reported how he had been received, and threatened to maim or kill some of them. The king struck a deal: the hostages would be freed if Hjalti and Gizurr went back to Iceland to promote Christianity.

## The Althing of 1000

The two returned in 1000 and gathered armed supporters on the road to the Althing. Christians and heathens were ready to fight. The Christians wanted Síðu-Hallr to proclaim their law, which would create two legal systems. Síðu-Hallr then agreed with the lawspeaker, the heathen chieftain Þorgeirr Ljósvetningagoði, that Þorgeirr himself would proclaim the law.

## Þorgeirr's decision

According to Ari Þorgilsson, in the Íslendingabók, Þorgeirr lay down under his cloak for a day and a night without speaking. Then he ordered everyone to the Law Rock and ruled that all Icelanders were to be baptised. Heathen sacrifice could go on in secret, but would be punished if there were witnesses. Neither side was satisfied, and both accepted it so as not to break the law. The scene of Þorgeirr throwing the idols into the waterfall Goðafoss looks like a 19th-century invention, since the falls' name is older.

## Afterwards

Hjalti later served King Ólafr Haraldsson. Snorri's account of the mission to Västergötland and Sweden is probably based on a report that Hjalti himself wrote after coming home.
