Grobiņa is a site in Latvia, near Liepāja, where there was a Scandinavian settlement. This piece uses two sources, an entry of an encyclopaedia in English and the file of the UNESCO tentative list. Interpretations and claims are marked.

## The site and the excavations

The settlement is near Liepāja, on the Latvian coast. The reading that it was a colony of Gotland is Nerman's, who excavated the site in 1929 and 1930. The cemeteries of the Vendel Age are dated to about 650 to 800 and are at Smukumi, Priediens and Porāni. Priediens has about 2000 mounds.

## The picture stone and the fort

In 1987 a picture stone was found, in Gotland style, from the second half of the 7th century. In the region there is also a hillfort, the Skābarža kalns.

## Seeburg and Rimbert

In the *Vita Ansgarii*, written around 854, Rimbert speaks of a place called "Seeburg". It is "usually" identified as Grobiņa. The troop numbers cited, 7,000 and 15,000, are claims of Rimbert, and not data confirmed in another way.

## The nomination

The UNESCO file states that the site would be the earliest Scandinavian settlement across the Baltic. That is the claim of the nomination, and this piece treats it as such.

## The place on the UNESCO list

The tentative list gathers the sites that a country intends to propose later as World Heritage. Being on it, therefore, does not mean that the site has already been accepted. The file on Grobiņa is the text the country wrote to defend the nomination, and for that reason it shows the view of those who propose.

## How the sources complete each other

The entry brings the location, Nerman's excavations, the cemeteries, the picture stone and Rimbert's reading. The UNESCO file brings the description of the site and the arguments of the nomination. One is the source of an encyclopaedia and the other is a nomination document, and for that reason each statement should be read with its origin in mind.

## In brief

In Grobiņa there are cemeteries with many mounds, a picture stone in the Gotland style and a hillfort. The link with Gotland comes from Nerman, the identification with Seeburg comes from Rimbert, and the title of earliest settlement comes from the nomination. Each of these ideas has its own origin, and none is a fact beyond discussion.

## Terms

A *settlement* is a place where a group establishes itself. The *Vendel Age* is a period before the Viking Age in Scandinavia. A *hillfort* is a fortification on a height. A *nomination* is the request to include a site in a list.

## What remains uncertain

Whether the settlement was a colony of Gotland is a reading of Nerman. Rimbert's troop numbers are claims of the author. The statement that it is the earliest settlement across the Baltic comes from the nomination. The entry says that Seeburg is "usually" identified as Grobiņa, and that leaves room for doubt.
