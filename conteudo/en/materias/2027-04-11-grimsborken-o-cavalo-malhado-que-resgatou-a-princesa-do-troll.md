Some Norwegian folktales have a horse cleverer than the hero. In "Grimsborken", the horse does the thinking and the lad does the riding. Dasent translated it into English as "Dapplegrim". The tale is in Asbjørnsen and Moe's *Norske Folkeeventyr* and is classified as ATU 531, "The Clever Horse".

## The horse

The youngest of twelve brothers leaves home. When he returns, his parents are dead and his brothers have divided the land, thinking him lost. They give him twelve mares as compensation. Each mare has a foal, and one is a sleek dapple-grey colt that talks. It advises the lad to kill the other foals so that it can have all the mares' milk for a year. The lad does so, and each year he finds the colt larger. After three years the colt, huge and radiant, offers to go with him. His name is Dapplegrim.

On the horse's advice, the lad trades the mares and foals for horseshoes, a golden saddle and a golden bridle.

## The first task: the princess

A troll has kidnapped the king's daughter, and the king promises her hand and half the kingdom to whoever rescues her. The lad rides Dapplegrim to the capital. Envious knights press the king to send him on the quest or have him killed. Dapplegrim asks for iron and silver horseshoes, carries the lad up the nearly vertical wall to the troll's cave, and the two take the princess before the troll can rise.

## The second task: the ridge

Back at court, the king says the lad must remove a ridge of rock that blocks the sun from his hall before he can marry the princess. Dapplegrim asks for heavier horseshoes and jumps on the ridge until it sinks.

## The third task: the other horse

Now the king demands a horse for the bride as splendid as the lad's. Dapplegrim asks for even larger shoes and supplies, and they travel to hell. After several adventures, Dapplegrim defeats the hellish horse. The lad bridles it, and the two horses are identical in every detail.

## Hide-and-seek

The last test is a game: the princess must hide twice and be found, and then the lad must hide twice without being found. The princess becomes a duck and then a loaf of bread, but Dapplegrim warns the lad each time. The lad becomes a tick in Dapplegrim's nostril and then a clod of earth between the hooves. Dapplegrim keeps the princess away, and she cannot find him. The couple ride to church and marry.

## In short

A talking horse, three tasks and a game of hide-and-seek: the tale shows a hero who wins because he listens to his companion's advice. The details are those of Dasent's version, which renders the text of Asbjørnsen and Moe, and other versions may differ.
