*Eiríks saga víðförla*, "the saga of Eirek the Traveller", is a late saga, probably from around 1300. The main source is the manuscript Skálholtsbók, and a scribe named Jón Þórðarson included it in the Flateyjarbók, according to a note in the translation. The saga merges the mythical Ódáinsakr, the "Deathless Acre", with the Christian heaven. Medieval Scandinavia (1993) doubts that any traditional Nordic material is preserved in it.

## The vow

Eirek is the son of King Þrándr, the first king of Þrándheimr. On Yule Eve he makes a vow: to seek Ódáinsakr, which Christians call Paradise.

## The road

He sails to Denmark and befriends the Danish prince, also called Eirek. They go on to Miklagarðr, Constantinople. The Greek king, who was fighting raiders, honours them, and after talks about God the two are baptised. With letters from the king and the patriarch, they head east, crossing India, of dark forests where stars can be seen by day and lumps of gold.

## The dragon

They come to a river with a stone bridge and an enormous dragon with open jaws. Eirek and a companion leap into its mouth. Beyond the smoke lies a sunlit land, with streams of honey and a tower hanging in the air, where a meal is laid out.

## The angel

An angel, young and bright, guards the gates of Paradise, with a wall of fire before them. He tells Eirek to go home and spread the word of God.

## The return

Eirek returns to Miklagarðr, after three years, and to Norway, after ten. In the eleventh winter he is taken by the spirit of God and never found again. The moral, according to the translation, is that faith in God is worth more than earthly fame.
