Þorsteins þáttr stangarhöggs, "the tale of Þorsteinn Staff-Struck", is a short account, much shorter than a full saga, set in Vopnafjörður. In reading order it comes after Þorsteins saga hvíta and Vápnfirðinga saga.

## The blow

Þorsteinn is the son of Thórarin of Sunnudalur, an old man, nearly blind, who was once a fierce Viking. He is a big, strong, even-tempered lad. Þord, farm manager for Víga-Bjarni Helgason at Hof, arranges a stallion fight. Þord's horse is losing, and he strikes Þorsteinn's. Þorsteinn hits back harder, and Þord strikes him in the face with a horse-staff and splits the skin above his brow. Þorsteinn asks them to keep it from his father. Þorhall and Þorvald, other workers of Bjarni's, give him the nickname "Staff-Struck".

## The revenge

His father calls him a coward. Þorsteinn goes to Hof, confronts Þord in the stable and kills him. He tells a woman that an ox gored Þord. Bjarni sees through it and outlaws him. In autumn Bjarni sends Þorhall and Þorvald to bring back Þorsteinn's head. Þorvald attacks with an axe and is killed with a short-sword. Þorhall dies too. Þorsteinn ties the bodies to a horse and sends them to Hof.

## The duel

Rannveig, Bjarni's wife, says everyone expects revenge. Bjarni goes alone to challenge Þorsteinn, who would rather leave on the first ship, but Bjarni refuses. They fight at a mound, and the shields are cut away piece by piece. Bjarni stops to drink water. When he ties his shoelace, Þorsteinn fetches better shields and a sword for him. The shields are cut away entirely, and Bjarni, without delivering the blow, says he is repaid for the three dead, if Þorsteinn will be loyal to him.

## The end

Þorsteinn serves Bjarni, at Hof, until his death. Thórarin, after the fight, tries to kill Bjarni with a knife and spends his last years at Sunnudalur.

## In short

It is a story of honour, of humiliation and of a peace made with swords. To strike in time with the duel, play [The duel at the mound](/game-the-duel-at-the-mound/).
