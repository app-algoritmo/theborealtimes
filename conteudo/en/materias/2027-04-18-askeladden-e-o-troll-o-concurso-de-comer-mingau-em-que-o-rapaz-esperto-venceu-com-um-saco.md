In many Norwegian folktales, the hero is the youngest son, poor and underestimated: Askeladden, whom Dasent translated into English as "Boots". This is one of the tales in which he needs neither a sword nor magic. He needs only a cool head and a bag.

## The debt and the forest

A poor, old farmer has three sons. To pay his debts, he sends the lads to hew wood in the forest. The two eldest flee when a troll threatens them. The youngest, Askeladden, goes next.

When the troll threatens him too, the boy takes a cheese from his bag and squeezes it until the whey runs out. He tells the troll he will squeeze him the same way. The troll pleads for mercy and offers to help hew. Together, they fell and cut a great deal of timber that day.

## The spring nobody can carry

In the evening, the troll takes the boy home. Askeladden finds the water pails too heavy to lift and offers to fetch the spring itself. The troll does not want to lose his spring, and prefers to fetch the water himself.

## The porridge match

The two cook porridge and agree to "eat a match" to see who can eat more. Secretly, Askeladden secretly hangs his bag and spoons much of the porridge into it instead of into his mouth. Then he slits the bag open.

When the troll says he cannot eat any more, Askeladden tells him to cut a hole in his belly, so he can keep eating. The troll does so and dies.

## The ending

The boy takes the silver and gold he finds in the hillside and goes home. The money goes a long way toward paying off the family's debt.

## What the tale shows

Trolls, in the tales of Asbjørnsen and Moe, are usually strong and foolish, and lose to the boy who tricks them with words and devices. Here there are three tests: the squeezed cheese, the spring he offers to carry and the eating contest. In all of them, Askeladden wins without strength, using a false threat, a promise that is too big and a bag. The troll is afraid of what he does not understand, and always believes the boy.

Wikipedia recalls that the Asbjørnsen and Moe collection is called *Norske Folkeeventyr* and that a tale of Askeladden was one of the first to open it. Here, the tale comes from Dasent's translation, which became very well known in English.

## In short

Askeladden beats the troll with a cheese, a spring he never carried and a bag hidden in the porridge match. The money he finds in the hillside pays off the family's debt. The tale has the structure of the Asbjørnsen and Moe tales: the poor youngest son, the foolish troll and victory through cleverness.
