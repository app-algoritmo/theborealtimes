The Gulating was, according to the source consulted, a yearly assembly of western Norway that met from about 900 to 1300. The place lies in the municipality of Gulen, in Vestland, on the west coast, north of Bergen. The original area of its law covered all of present-day Vestland.

## Origin

The assembly site was established early in the tenth century. The unification of Norway into a single kingdom took place, according to the article, from 900 to 1030. King Hákon the Good (935 to 961) took an active part in the assemblies at Gulen, and under his rule Rogaland, Agder and Sunnmøre became part of the Gulating area, and Valdres and Hallingdal were added later. The assembly, at first, was open to all free farmers.

## The law

The Older Gulating Law is the oldest record of Norwegian law and may have first been created in the reign of Olaf the Peaceful (1066 to 1093). The Younger Gulating Law replaced it in 1267 and lasted until 1274. In that year, Magnus Lagabøte imposed his "Law of the Realm", which incorporated 130 chapters from the Gulating law.

## The manuscripts

The older law survives in seventeen manuscripts. The most complete, DonVar 137 4to, the Codex Rantzovianus, is from about 1260 and is in the Royal Danish Library.

## Dates in brief

The article allows a timeline to be built: about 900, the annual assembly begins; in the early tenth century, the site is established; from 935 to 961, Hákon the Good reigns; from 1066 to 1093, Olaf the Peaceful; around 1260, the most complete manuscript; in 1267, the new law replaces the old; in 1274, Magnus's Law of the Realm; around 1300, the assembly stops meeting. In the late thirteenth century, according to the article, he also had the existing law put into writing.

## A collective fee

Under sections 218 to 228, killing an heir to a property carried a collective compensation of 189 cattle, divided among those responsible. That provision shows that the law provided for payment in cattle for a death, and not only punishment.

## What remains uncertain

The article says the older law "may" have been created in the reign of Olaf the Peaceful, that is, the date is debated. It does not report how many representatives went to the assembly or how votes were taken. The notion that all free farmers took part holds for the beginning, and not necessarily for the whole four-century period.

## A prudent reading

Compensation for death, measured in cattle, shows a society in which justice ran through fixed sums. But the article does not describe how each amount was reached, and it is best not to imagine more than the text allows.
