Olav Ugjæva, in Old Norse Ólafr úgæfa, died in 1169. His given name was Olav Gudbrandsson, and the nickname means "the unlucky". He was the son of Gudbrand Skavhoggsson and Maria Øysteinsdotter, and through his mother the grandson of King Eystein I and Ingebjørg Guttormsdatter. He grew up in Oppland, in eastern Norway, fostered by Sigurd Agnhatt.

## A country in the earl's hands

In the late 1160s, Norway was governed by Earl Erling Skakke, during the minority of his son, King Magnus Erlingsson. Anyone with royal blood from another line was a threat, and Olav had it, through his mother.

## The proclamation of 1166

In 1166, Sigurd Agnhatt and Olav raised a force in Oppland and had Olav proclaimed king, while Erling was in Denmark. The young man was not an experienced warrior. He had the support of a region, of men his foster-father knew.

## Rydjokul, 2 February 1167

Olav's men set an ambush for Erling at Rydjokul, in Sørum, on 2 February 1167. Erling was wounded, but barely escaped. The sagas say Olav was unlucky not to win, and that is what gave him the nickname Ugjæva.

## Stanger, 1168

In 1168, Olav's troops moved to the Oslofjord area and were defeated at the Battle of Stanger, near Våler in Østfold. Sigurd Agnhatt died. Olav fled to Denmark. The page consulted does not say who commanded the winning side.

## Death in Denmark

In 1169, Olav fell ill and died in Denmark. Heimskringla and Fagrskinna agree on the country but differ on the town: Århus or Ålborg.

## What can be said

What is recorded is little: a proclamation, an ambush, a defeat, an exile and an illness. We do not know what Olav was like or what he himself thought of his fate. The nickname comes from the sagas, and is the judgement of those who wrote them: he almost won, but did not win.
