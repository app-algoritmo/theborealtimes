In the history of voyages, the one remembered is usually the one who lands. The Greenlanders' Saga keeps an exception: Bjarni Herjólfsson, the captain who saw an unknown coast in the west and preferred not to set foot on it, because he wanted to get home.

## The wrong course

According to the saga, Bjarni was a merchant based in Norway who visited his father in Iceland every summer. In 986 he reached Iceland expecting to find his parents and discovered that his father, Herjólfr, had gone with Erik the Red to Greenland. Bjarni decided to follow him with his crew. He had no map or compass, and a storm blew him off course.

## Three lands, no landing

First he saw a mountainous, wooded land that was not Greenland. The crew begged him to stop and explore it, but he refused. Later, having regained his course, he reported low hills covered in forest farther west. Professor Tryggvi Oleson argued that the three lands Bjarni saw were most likely Newfoundland, Labrador and Baffin Island. Bjarni would not explore any of them, because he was in a hurry to see his parents.

## Back to his own

He reached Greenland and settled with his father at Herjólfsnes. He told what he had seen, but few took an interest, until, after his father's death, he returned to Norway. There Earl Eirik chided him for not investigating the lands he had seen, though others celebrated his discoveries.

## Leif's ship

Leif Eriksson, son of Erik the Red, bought Bjarni's ship, hired a crew of 35 men and retraced the route. This voyage is thought to have produced the Viking settlement at L'Anse aux Meadows, in Newfoundland. So the voyage Bjarni made without meaning to opened the way for another, made on purpose.

## In short

Bjarni Herjólfsson, in 986, missed his course to Greenland, sighted three lands in the west and did not land. The Greenlanders' Saga presents him as the one who showed the way that Leif later followed.

## Terms

The *Greenlanders' Saga* is an Icelandic saga about the settlement of Greenland and the voyages west. *Herjólfsnes* is the settlement of Bjarni's father in Greenland. *Earl* translates *jarl*, the noble chief of Norway.

## What remains uncertain

The saga was written centuries after the events, and the date of 985 or 986 and the identification of the three lands are interpretations. Oleson's proposal is only one among others, and the account of the voyage, without maps or instruments, does not allow the route to be reconstructed. It is also possible that the account of Bjarni serves, in the saga, to explain where Leif got the idea for his voyage, rather than being a faithful memory of a real voyage.
