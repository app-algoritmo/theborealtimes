Skule Bårdsson was born around 1189 and was a half-brother of King Inge Bårdsson. In Inge's last years, the king made him an earl (jarl). When the king died, in 1217, Håkon, aged 13, was chosen king over Skule's candidate. The two shared power, and most of the real power stayed in Skule's hands.

## The king's father-in-law

To reconcile the rivals, Skule's daughter Margrete married Håkon in 1225. That made King Håkon Skule's son-in-law, but the tension only grew. In 1237 Skule received the title of duke (hertug), the first in Norway.

## The proclamation

In 1239 his supporters proclaimed him king at the Øyrating, the traditional assembly of Trøndelag. The party that backed him became known as the Vårbelger. Civil war broke out again. Skule won a battle at Låka, in Nannestad, but lost at Oslo and had to fall back.

## Elgeseter

After the defeat, Skule sought refuge in Elgeseter priory, in Nidaros. On 24 May 1240, Håkon's men burned the place and killed him. The duke's death ended the era of Norway's civil wars.

## Who backed him

Snorri Sturluson was an ally of Skule. The two great parties of the civil wars were eventually merged into two, the Baglers and the Birkebeiners. The sources that cover this period include the Heimskringla, the Fagrskinna, the Morkinskinna and Orkneyinga saga. The reign of Håkon IV is told in Håkon Håkonssons saga, written in the 13th century by the Icelander Sturla Þórðarson, which explains why the version that reached us is that of the winning side.

## What remains

Skule climbed almost every step: earl, duke, the king's father-in-law and, briefly, a proclaimed king. He was the last great name of Norway's civil wars.
